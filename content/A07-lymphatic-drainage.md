# A07 · Lymphatic drainage of the liver and biliary tree

**Level:** MRCP  **Prerequisites:** A03  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

## Learning outcomes
1. Describe the three hepatic lymphatic compartments and the relative volume each carries.
2. Name the nodal stations of the hepatoduodenal ligament and the coeliac axis, and the downstream drainage to the thoracic duct.
3. Explain how sinusoidal hypertension increases lymph production and contributes to ascites.
4. Relate the anatomy to nodal staging in hepatobiliary and pancreatic cancer.

## 1 · Compartments and volume

Buttons: **"Portal tract lymphatics"** [NEEDS ASSET] · **"Sublobular vessels along the hepatic veins"** [NEEDS ASSET] · **"Capsular network"** [NEEDS ASSET].

### 1.1 Three compartments
- The hepatic lymphatic system is divided into **portal**, **sublobular** and **superficial (capsular)** vessels. Over 80% of hepatic lymph drains through the portal lymphatics. [V: Ohtani & Ohtani 2008, review]
- The liver produces a large volume of lymph, estimated at 25–50% of the flow in the thoracic duct. [V: Ohtani & Ohtani 2008]

### 1.2 Origin of lymph
- Lymph forms from fluid that leaves the sinusoids into the space of Disse and passes into the space of Mall (around portal tracts) and the sublobular spaces. [T; Mall's space V: Lautt 1985 in P01 for context]
- Sinusoids are highly permeable (fenestrated endothelium with no basement membrane; A11), so hepatic lymph has a protein content close to plasma. [T; permeability V: Poisson 2017 in A11]

## 2 · Routes and nodes

**Atlas view.** [NEEDS ASSET]. Structures for orientation: `portal-trunk`, `proper-hepatic-artery`, `common-bile-duct`, `coeliac-trunk`, `gallbladder`, `ivc`.

### 2.1 Drainage routes
- **Portal and most of the deep lymph** drains along the portal pedicles to nodes of the hepatoduodenal ligament (hepatic hilum, cystic, portal, and then common hepatic artery and coeliac nodes) and on to the cisterna chyli and thoracic duct. [T]
- **Along the hepatic veins** to the retrohepatic IVC and through the diaphragm to nodes at the lower mediastinum (paracardiac/phrenic). [T]
- **Capsular lymph** from the anterior surface and falciform ligament drains to diaphragmatic and internal mammary nodes; the bare area drains to the posterior mediastinum. [T]

### 2.2 Gallbladder and bile ducts
- The gallbladder drains through the cystic node (near the cystic duct-CHD junction) to the hilar and portal nodes. The extrahepatic bile duct drains to the hepatoduodenal ligament, retropancreatic and coeliac nodes. [T]

## 3 · Clinical correlation

### 3.1 Ascites and lymph overflow
- In sinusoidal portal hypertension (cirrhosis), hepatic sinusoidal pressure rises and lymph production increases; when flow exceeds thoracic duct capacity, fluid weeps from the capsule as ascites. [T; mechanism detailed in P09]
- Ascites management guidance is in the EASL decompensated cirrhosis CPG 2018; **it is over 5 years old, and no update was found in this search.** [V: existence and year]

### 3.2 Nodal staging
- Regional lymph nodes are staged in intrahepatic and perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, gallbladder cancer and pancreatic head cancer; hepatoduodenal ligament and coeliac nodes are the first stations. [T; staging edition **[UNVERIFIED]**, not looked up]
- Cross-sectional imaging identifies nodal enlargement but cannot exclude metastasis in normal-sized nodes. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

### 3.3 Portacaval nodes
- Benign enlargement of portacaval nodes is frequent in chronic hepatitis and cirrhosis and should not be mistaken for metastasis. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 4 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which compartment carries most hepatic lymph?
A. Capsular  B. Sublobular  C. Portal  D. Diaphragmatic  E. Retroperitoneal
**Answer: C.** More than 80% drains through portal lymphatics (Ohtani & Ohtani 2008).

**Q2.** What proportion of thoracic duct flow is estimated to come from the liver?
A. 1–5%  B. 5–10%  C. 10–20%  D. 25–50%  E. 80–90%
**Answer: D.** The liver contributes an estimated 25–50% of thoracic duct lymph.

**Q3.** The first nodal station of the gallbladder is the:
A. Coeliac node  B. Cystic node  C. Para-aortic node  D. Iliac node  E. Mesenteric node
**Answer: B.** The cystic node drains onward to the hepatoduodenal ligament nodes.

**Q4.** Which explains the raised lymph production in cirrhosis?
A. Raised oncotic pressure  B. Increased sinusoidal pressure  C. Bile duct obstruction  D. Reduced portal flow  E. Hepatic vein dilation
**Answer: B.** Sinusoidal hypertension increases fluid filtration into the space of Disse and lymph formation.

**Q5.** An enlarged portacaval node on CT in a patient with chronic hepatitis C is most likely:
A. Metastatic  B. Reactive  C. Lymphoma  D. Tuberculous  E. Amyloid
**Answer: B.** Reactive nodes are common in chronic liver disease. **[T; verify against a radiology source before publishing.]**
