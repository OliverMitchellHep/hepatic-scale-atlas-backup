# A02 · Couinaud segments and vascular supply

**Level:** student → MRCP  **Prerequisite:** A01 (gross structure)  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags used below: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search this session; **[T]** standard textbook anatomy (Gray's, Blumgart, Sherlock), not opened this session, so treat as unverified until you have spot-checked it; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed and should be cut or checked before release.

## Learning outcomes
1. Name the nine segments (I, II, III, IVa, IVb, V–VIII) and place each by its hepatic vein and portal pedicle boundaries.
2. Explain why portal pedicles run through the centre of segments and hepatic veins run between them.
3. Use Brisbane 2000 / Tokyo 2020 terms (hemiliver, section, sector, segment) without mixing them up.
4. State the common portal, arterial and hepatic venous variants and their published prevalence.
5. Apply the anatomy to segment VIII resection, caudate hypertrophy in Budd–Chiari syndrome, and resection in portal hypertension.

## 1 · The principle: two systems that interdigitate

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior. `parenchyma` Ghost, `portal` On, `hepaticVein` On, `artery` Off, `biliary` Off.
Buttons: **"Show portal pedicles only"** (`hepaticVein` Off) · **"Show hepatic veins only"** (`portal` Off) · **"Show both"**.
Highlight: `right-portal-vein`, `left-portal-vein`, `right-hepatic-vein`, `middle-hepatic-vein`, `left-hepatic-vein`.

### 1.1 Pedicles inside, veins between
- The portal vein, hepatic artery and bile duct travel together in a Glisson sheath, so a segment is defined by one pedicle. [V: Strasberg 2000; Bismuth 1982]
- Hepatic veins run in the planes between territories, so each vein drains parts of two neighbouring segments. [V: Bismuth 1982]
- Bismuth's scheme: four sectors, eight segments, defined by portal pedicle distribution and hepatic vein position. [V]

### 1.2 Two sorts of "lobe"
- **Anatomical:** the falciform ligament separates the left lateral section (II, III) from IV. [T]
- **Functional:** the main portal fissure (Cantlie's line, from the gallbladder fossa to the IVC, containing the middle hepatic vein) separates right and left hemilivers. Segment IV therefore belongs to the left hemiliver. [T]

### 1.3 Nomenclature
| Order | Brisbane 2000 term | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Hemiliver (right, left) | Right: V–VIII. Left: II–IV. |
| 2nd | **Section** (hepatic artery / bile duct watershed); **sector** (portal vein watershed) | Right anterior, right posterior, left medial, left lateral |
| 3rd | Segment | Couinaud segments |

- Brisbane keeps "segment" for the third order only and adds "section" for the second order defined by arterial and biliary watersheds. "Sector" is retained for the portal-vein watershed. [V: Strasberg 2000]
- The left is where the two differ: the left portal vein gives an umbilical branch to III and IV and a branch to II, whereas the left artery and duct divide into a medial branch (IV) and a lateral branch (II, III). [V: Strasberg 2000]
- Tokyo 2020 updates Brisbane by defining anatomy and resections Brisbane left undefined. [V: Wakabayashi 2022]

## 2 · The segments

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior. `segments` On, `parenchyma` Solid, `portal` and `hepaticVein` Ghost.

Not in the atlas yet: Cantlie's line and the three fissure planes as overlays [NEEDS ASSET]; ligamentum venosum [NEEDS ASSET].

### 2.1 Right hemiliver
- **Right posterior section: VI (inferior), VII (superior).** Separated from the anterior section by the right hepatic vein in the right intersectoral plane. [T]
- **Right anterior section: V (inferior), VIII (superior).** Bounded medially by the middle hepatic vein. [T]
- The upper/lower split runs in a transverse plane through the right and left portal branches (Couinaud's plane). [T]

### 2.2 Left hemiliver
- **Left lateral section: II (superior), III (inferior).** Separated by the left hepatic vein. [T]
- **Left medial section: IV**, divided into **IVa** (superior) and **IVb** (inferior) by the same transverse plane. Bounded on the left by the umbilical fissure (ligamentum teres, falciform). [T]
- Brisbane counts these as segments 1–9 once IV is divided. [V: Strasberg 2000]

### 2.3 Segment I (caudate)
- Sits between the IVC and the ligamentum venosum. It receives portal and arterial branches from both right and left pedicles, and drains by short veins directly into the IVC, independent of the three main hepatic veins. [T; drainage V: Bargalló 2003]
- This autonomy is why it hypertrophies when the main hepatic veins are occluded (Section 6.2).

## 3 · Portal venous supply

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior then inferior. `portal` On, `parenchyma` Ghost, `pancreas` On, `spleen` On.
Buttons: **"Trace the portal trunk"** (highlight `superior-mesenteric-vein`, `splenic-vein`, `portal-trunk`) · **"Intrahepatic branching"** (highlight `right-anterior-portal-branch`, `right-posterior-portal-branch`, `umbilical-portion`, `left-portal-vein`) · **"Show tributaries"** (`inferior-mesenteric-vein`, `left-gastric-vein`).
Variant (trifurcation; right posterior branch as first branch): [NEEDS ASSET].

### 3.1 Extrahepatic
- The portal trunk forms behind the pancreatic neck from the superior mesenteric and splenic veins; the inferior mesenteric vein joins one of them or the confluence (variable). [T]
- It ascends in the hepatoduodenal ligament, posterior to the bile duct and hepatic artery, and divides at the porta hepatis. [T]

### 3.2 Intrahepatic
- **Right portal vein** → right anterior branch (V, VIII) and right posterior branch (VI, VII). [T]
- **Left portal vein**: transverse part, then the umbilical portion turns anteriorly at the umbilical fissure and supplies III and IV; a branch supplies II; small branches supply the caudate. [T; III/IV/II pattern V: Strasberg 2000]

### 3.3 Variants
- In a series of 200 patients with contrast-enhanced imaging, standard branching was seen in 65%, portal trifurcation in 9% and a right posterior portal vein arising as the first branch of the main portal vein in 13%. [V: Covey 2004, retrospective imaging series]
- Why it matters: when the right posterior branch arises separately, ligating or embolising "the right portal vein" can spare or injure it unexpectedly. This is inference from the anatomy, not a cited finding.

## 4 · Hepatic arterial supply

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior. `artery` On, `portal` Ghost, `parenchyma` Ghost, `pancreas` Ghost, `stomach` Ghost.
Buttons: **"Coeliac to segmental arteries"** (highlight `coeliac-trunk`, `common-hepatic-artery`, `proper-hepatic-artery`, `right-hepatic-artery`, `left-hepatic-artery`, `segmental-hepatic-artery`) · **"Gastroduodenal artery"** (`gastroduodenal-artery`, `superior-pancreaticoduodenal-artery`) · **"Cystic artery"** (`cystic-artery`, `gallbladder`).
Variants (replaced RHA from SMA; replaced/accessory LHA from left gastric): [NEEDS ASSET]. `superior-mesenteric-artery` and `left-gastric-artery` exist for the origin.

### 4.1 Standard pattern
- Coeliac trunk → common hepatic artery → gastroduodenal artery and proper hepatic artery → right and left hepatic arteries. The cystic artery usually arises from the right. [T]
- Arterial branches follow the portal pedicles, so the arterial and portal territories coincide. [V: Strasberg 2000]

### 4.2 Variants
- In 1,000 liver donors (Hiatt 1994), the standard pattern was seen in about 76%. A replaced or accessory right hepatic artery from the SMA occurred in 10.6% (106 of 1,000). The series also describes replaced or accessory left hepatic arteries from the left gastric artery. [V: Hiatt 1994; 10.6% and ~76% from search summaries of the paper, check against the full text; left gastric figure not confirmed, so none is given]
- A replaced right hepatic artery from the SMA typically runs behind the portal vein and along the right side of the bile duct. [T]
- Michels' 1966 ten-type classification is the older scheme; Hiatt collapsed it to six types. [V]

## 5 · Hepatic venous outflow

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior and posterior. `hepaticVein` On, `portal` Ghost, `parenchyma` Ghost.
Buttons: **"Three hepatic veins"** (highlight `right-hepatic-vein`, `middle-hepatic-vein`, `left-hepatic-vein`) · **"Common trunk"** (`middle-and-left-hepatic-vein-trunk`) · **"Caudate veins"** (`caudate-veins`, `ivc`; camera posterior).
Inferior right hepatic vein: [NEEDS ASSET].

### 5.1 Territories
- **Right hepatic vein:** VI and VII, with parts of V and VIII. **Middle:** IV, V, VIII, in Cantlie's line. **Left:** II and III, often joined to the middle vein before entering the IVC. [T]
- The atlas models the middle and left veins joining in a short common trunk and entering the anterolateral IVC just below the caval opening.

### 5.2 Short hepatic veins
- Small veins, including the caudate veins, drain directly into the retrohepatic IVC just below the main vein ostia. The caudate vein is the largest of them. [V: Bargalló 2003; T]

### 5.3 Variants
- An inferior right hepatic vein draining VI/VII directly to the IVC is frequent, and when large it may replace much of the right hepatic vein's territory. [V: Nakamura & Tsuzuki 1981, 83 autopsies; prevalence figure not confirmed, so none given, **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 6 · Clinical correlation

### 6.1 Why segment VIII resection is different
- Segment VIII is deep and dome-shaped, lies beneath the diaphragm between the middle and right hepatic veins, and abuts the IVC and hepatic vein confluence. [V: search summaries of laparoscopic series; T]
- It has few surface landmarks and a complex, variable pedicle pattern; a 27-patient CT-portography series describes its multiple subsegmental portal branches. [V: Cho 2000]
- Practical result: the Glissonean pedicle to VIII is hard to identify, so surgeons often use a hepatic-vein-guided approach along the middle hepatic vein. [V: Monden 2023, technique series]
- The atlas can show both bounding veins with `segment-viii` highlighted; the dome view needs the diaphragm layer (`diaphragm` Ghost).

### 6.2 Caudate hypertrophy in Budd–Chiari syndrome
- Occlusion of the main hepatic veins leaves the caudate's separate short veins draining to the IVC. In Budd–Chiari syndrome the caudate vein is often enlarged and visible on sonography (half of 16 BCS patients versus 7.5% of controls). [V: Bargalló 2003, small case-control study]
- Caudate enlargement is therefore a sign of hepatic venous outflow obstruction on cross-sectional imaging, and it can compress the IVC. [T]
- Current guidance: **EASL vascular liver disease CPG 2025 supersedes the 2016 version**; the 2016 document (J Hepatol 64:179) is over 5 years old and should not be cited for management. [V: existence of 2025 update; content not read]

### 6.3 Resection and portal hypertension
- Clinically significant portal hypertension is defined by HVPG ≥10 mmHg. [V: Baveno VII 2022. **Baveno VIII was published online 19 Aug 2026 and supersedes it; the definition has not been rechecked against VIII.**]
- The EASL HCC guideline (2025) is reported to say that clinically significant portal hypertension with Child–Pugh B cirrhosis is an absolute contraindication to major resection (more than two segments), while limited minimally invasive resection is not absolutely contraindicated. [Guideline 2025; wording taken from secondary summaries, **grade [UNVERIFIED]**; check the CPG text]
- Anatomical link: segmental resection preserves the remnant's inflow and outflow, so it suits patients with low reserve. [T]

## 7 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which structure lies in the main portal fissure (Cantlie's line)?
A. Right hepatic vein  B. Middle hepatic vein  C. Left hepatic vein  D. Ligamentum venosum  E. Umbilical portion of the left portal vein
**Answer: B.** The middle hepatic vein runs in the main portal fissure between the right and left hemilivers, draining parts of both. The right vein lies in the right intersectoral plane and the left vein between II and III. The umbilical portion lies in the umbilical fissure.

**Q2.** A 32-year-old with Budd–Chiari syndrome has an enlarged caudate lobe on CT. Which statement best explains this?
A. The caudate is supplied only by the right hepatic artery
B. The caudate drains through short veins directly into the IVC, so its outflow is preserved
C. The caudate is supplied by a separate portal vein from the splenic vein
D. The caudate lies within the right hemiliver and is drained by the right hepatic vein
E. The caudate has no venous drainage
**Answer: B.** Segment I drains directly to the retrohepatic IVC via the caudate veins, which bypass the occluded main hepatic veins. It receives portal and arterial branches from both sides, so A and D are wrong.

**Q3.** Pre-hepatectomy CT shows an artery arising from the SMA and running behind the portal vein into the right lobe. What is this, and roughly how common is it?
A. Cystic artery, rare  B. Replaced right hepatic artery, about 10% in a large donor series  C. Left gastric artery, about 50%  D. Gastroduodenal artery, universal  E. Accessory left hepatic artery, about 90%
**Answer: B.** A replaced or accessory right hepatic artery from the SMA was found in 10.6% of 1,000 donors (Hiatt 1994). It must be identified and preserved because it may be the sole right arterial supply.

**Q4.** In Brisbane 2000 terminology, resection of segments II and III is called:
A. Left hepatectomy  B. Left medial sectionectomy  C. Left lateral sectionectomy  D. Segmentectomy IV  E. Extended left hepatectomy
**Answer: C.** II and III form the left lateral section. Left hepatectomy removes II–IV (with or without I); the left medial section is IV (4a and 4b).

**Q5.** A 60-year-old with Child–Pugh B cirrhosis, HVPG 12 mmHg and a single 3 cm HCC in segment VIII is considered for surgery. Which approach is most consistent with EASL 2025 HCC guidance as summarised above?
A. Right hepatectomy  B. Extended right hepatectomy  C. Limited (segmental) resection via a minimally invasive approach, or an alternative such as transplant or locoregional therapy, after MDT review  D. No treatment  E. Right hepatectomy after portal vein ligation
**Answer: C.** Clinically significant portal hypertension with Child–Pugh B is described as an absolute contraindication to major resection, not to limited resection. **[UNVERIFIED wording: confirm against the CPG before publishing this item.]**
