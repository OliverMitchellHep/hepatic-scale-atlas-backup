# P08 · Bilirubin, jaundice and the cholestatic pattern

**Level:** student → MRCP  **Prerequisites:** P03  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook physiology, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Bile acid transport is in P03 and is not repeated.

## Learning outcomes
1. Trace bilirubin from haem to excretion and locate each step.
2. Classify jaundice as pre-hepatic, hepatic and post-hepatic and predict the biochemistry.
3. Distinguish hepatocellular from cholestatic liver test patterns.
4. Explain why conjugated, but not unconjugated, bilirubin appears in urine.

## 1 · Bilirubin metabolism

**Atlas view.** Tier 3/4. `hepatocytes` On, `blood` On, `bile` On; tier 4 `transporters` On.
Buttons: **"Uptake at the sinusoidal face"** (highlight `sinusoidal-membrane`, `basolateral-transporter`) · **"Conjugation in the ER"** (`endoplasmic-reticulum`) · **"Excretion at the canaliculus"** (`canalicular-transporter`, `bile-canaliculus`).

### 1.1 Steps
- Haem from senescent red cells is converted to biliverdin and then unconjugated bilirubin, which is albumin-bound in plasma. [T]
- Hepatocytes take up bilirubin, conjugate it with glucuronic acid (UGT1A1, in the ER), and export it into the canaliculus by MRP2 (P03). [T; MRP2 role V: Boyer 2013]
- In the gut, bacteria convert bilirubin to urobilinogen; some is reabsorbed and excreted in urine, and the rest is converted to stercobilin. [T]

### 1.2 Water solubility
- Conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and can be filtered by the kidney; unconjugated bilirubin is albumin-bound and is not. [T]

## 2 · Patterns of jaundice

**Atlas view.** Tier 0. Pathology: biliary obstruction (`state.path.bil`: cause, stent).
Buttons: **"Common bile duct stone"** (highlight `common-bile-duct-stone`) · **"Benign stricture"** (`benign-cbd-stricture`) · **"Perihilar tumour"** (cholangiocarcinoma controls in the panel) · **"Clear"**.

### 2.1 Classification
| Type | Mechanism | Bilirubin | Urine bilirubin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-hepatic | Haemolysis, ineffective erythropoiesis | Unconjugated | Absent |
| Hepatic | Impaired uptake, conjugation or excretion | Mixed | Present if conjugated raised |
| Post-hepatic | Obstruction to bile flow | Conjugated | Present |
[T]

### 2.2 Inherited disorders
- **Gilbert syndrome** (UGT1A1 promoter variant): mild unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, prevalence around 10% in some sources and 2–12% in others, with marked inter-ethnic variation. [V: Fevery 2008; secondary summaries of the range]
- **Crigler–Najjar** (severe UGT1A1 deficiency) and **Dubin–Johnson** (MRP2) are rare. [T; P03]

## 3 · Liver test patterns

### 3.1 Interpretation
- Interpret abnormal liver tests only after reviewing previous results, history and clinical context; the extent of abnormality is not necessarily a guide to significance. [V: BSG 2018 via search summary]
- Single abnormal tests warrant further investigation in the absence of a cause; 84% of tests remain abnormal on retesting at one month. [V: BSG 2018 via search summary; **guideline is over 5 years old**, check for an update]

### 3.2 Hepatocellular vs cholestatic
- Hepatocellular: predominant ALT/AST rise; cholestatic: predominant ALP/GGT rise. [T; R factor thresholds **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which enzyme conjugates bilirubin?
A. CYP2E1  B. UGT1A1  C. Glutathione S-transferase  D. BSEP  E. MRP2
**Answer: B.** MRP2 exports the conjugate.

**Q2.** A patient has jaundice, dark urine and pale stools. The bilirubin is most likely:
A. Unconjugated  B. Conjugated  C. Delta only  D. Normal  E. Albumin-bound only
**Answer: B.** Conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and appears in urine.

**Q3.** Which pattern suggests a cholestatic picture?
A. ALT 800, ALP normal  B. ALP 600, GGT raised, ALT 60  C. Albumin low only  D. INR raised only  E. Bilirubin normal
**Answer: B.**

**Q4.** Gilbert syndrome is characterised by:
A. Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia  B. Mild unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia  C. Cholestasis  D. Raised ALT  E. Haemolysis
**Answer: B.**

**Q5.** In the 2018 BSG guidance summarised here, single abnormal liver tests:
A. Can be ignored  B. Warrant further investigation in the absence of a cause  C. Need biopsy  D. Resolve in most cases within a month  E. Require MRCP
**Answer: B.** 84% remain abnormal at one month. [Guideline over 5 years old.]
