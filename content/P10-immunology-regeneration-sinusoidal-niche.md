# P10 · Immunology, regeneration and the sinusoidal niche

**Level:** registrar  **Prerequisites:** A11, P02  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook physiology/immunology, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Cell types are defined in A11.

## Learning outcomes
1. Describe the liver's dual role as a tolerogenic and a frontline immune organ.
2. Describe the sequence of regeneration after resection and its limits in cirrhosis.
3. Explain the role of stellate cells and the sinusoidal niche in fibrosis and regression.
4. Link these to post-hepatectomy liver failure and transplant tolerance.

## 1 · Immune function

**Atlas view.** Tier 3. `cells` On, `endothelium` On, `blood` On.

### 1.1 Position
- The liver is a frontline immune tissue positioned to detect pathogens entering from the gut, designed to capture and clear bacteria, viruses and macromolecules. Its default immune state is anti-inflammatory and tolerant, but it can mount rapid, robust responses. [V: Kubes & Jenne 2018, review]

### 1.2 Kupffer cells and LSECs
- Kupffer cells and LSECs clear particles; LSECs also present antigen and contribute to tolerance. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 2 · Regeneration

**Atlas view.** Tier 0. Resection simulation (`planned-resection`, `transection-surface-remnant`, `resection-specimen`).
Buttons: **"Plan a resection"** · **"Show the remnant"**.

### 2.1 Sequence
- After partial hepatectomy, hepatocytes replicate; other cell types follow. If hepatocyte or cholangiocyte proliferation is seriously impaired, each can transdifferentiate into the other and act as a facultative stem cell. [V: Michalopoulos & Bhushan 2021, review]
- In chronic disease, loss of hepatocytes triggers compensatory proliferation of survivors, exposing them to genotoxic injury that may lead to neoplasia. [V: Michalopoulos & Bhushan 2021]

### 2.2 Limits
- Regeneration is impaired in cirrhosis, steatosis and after chemotherapy, hence the larger FLR thresholds (A08). [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 3 · The sinusoidal niche and fibrosis

### 3.1 Stellate cells and capillarisation
- Capillarisation precedes fibrosis and is permissive for stellate cell activation; restoring LSEC differentiation promotes stellate cell quiescence and fibrosis regression in experiments. [V: DeLeve 2015; experimental data]

### 3.2 Regression
- Fibrosis can regress when the cause is removed. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**, check current guidance]

## 4 · Clinical correlation

- **Post-hepatectomy liver failure:** the ISGLS definition and grades (A08). [V: Rahbari 2011]
- **Transplant tolerance:** the tolerogenic liver environment contributes to lower rejection rates than other solid organs. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]
- **Autoimmune disease:** interface hepatitis is a lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate breaching the limiting plate (A11). [T]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** The liver's default immune state is:
A. Pro-inflammatory  B. Anti-inflammatory and tolerant  C. Immunodeficient  D. Cytotoxic  E. Absent
**Answer: B.** It can still respond rapidly. (Kubes & Jenne 2018.)

**Q2.** In severely impaired proliferation, hepatocytes and cholangiocytes can:
A. Not repair  B. Transdifferentiate into each other  C. Become fibroblasts  D. Become Kupffer cells  E. Enter apoptosis only
**Answer: B.** (Michalopoulos & Bhushan 2021.)

**Q3.** Capillarisation of sinusoids:
A. Follows fibrosis  B. Precedes fibrosis and permits stellate cell activation  C. Is irrelevant  D. Prevents fibrosis  E. Increases fenestrae
**Answer: B.** (DeLeve 2015.)

**Q4.** ISGLS PHLF is defined by:
A. Raised ALT  B. Raised INR and hyperbilirubinaemia on or after day 5  C. Fever  D. Bile leak  E. Ascites
**Answer: B.**

**Q5.** Which cells clear particles from portal blood?
A. Hepatocytes only  B. Kupffer cells and LSECs  C. Cholangiocytes  D. Stellate cells only  E. Erythrocytes
**Answer: B.** **[T]**
