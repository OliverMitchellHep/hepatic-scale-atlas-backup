# A06 · Innervation of the liver and biliary tree

**Level:** MRCP  **Prerequisites:** A01, A04  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook anatomy/physiology, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. The atlas does not model nerves; every nerve view below is [NEEDS ASSET].

## Learning outcomes
1. Describe the sympathetic, parasympathetic and sensory supply of the liver, gallbladder and biliary tree and where the fibres run.
2. Explain where sensory fibres end in the liver and why the parenchyma itself is painless.
3. Explain referred pain from the liver, gallbladder and diaphragm.
4. Apply the anatomy to coeliac plexus neurolysis and to the denervated liver.

## 1 · Efferent supply

**Atlas view.** Tier 0. `artery` On, `portal` Ghost, `biliary` Ghost, `parenchyma` Ghost, `esophagus` On, `diaphragm` Ghost. Nerves: [NEEDS ASSET: coeliac plexus, hepatic plexus, anterior and posterior vagal trunks]. Real structures for orientation: `coeliac-trunk`, `common-hepatic-artery`, `proper-hepatic-artery`, `oesophageal-hiatus`, `median-arcuate-ligament`.
Buttons: **"Coeliac plexus and hepatic plexus"** [NEEDS ASSET] · **"Vagal trunks at the hiatus"** [NEEDS ASSET].

### 1.1 Sympathetic
- Preganglionic fibres from the lower thoracic cord (about T7–T10) pass through the greater splanchnic nerves to the coeliac ganglia; postganglionic fibres form the **hepatic plexus** around the hepatic artery and portal vein. [T]
- Sympathetic activity stimulates hepatic glucose production and reduces hepatic blood flow through arterial and portal vasoconstriction. [V: Rui 2014 for the stimulation of gluconeogenesis; blood flow: T]

### 1.2 Parasympathetic
- The anterior vagal trunk gives a hepatic branch through the lesser omentum to the hepatic plexus and the biliary tree. [T]
- Vagal activity suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis. [V: Rui 2014]

### 1.3 Gallbladder and sphincter of Oddi
- Vagal fibres and CCK stimulate gallbladder contraction and sphincter relaxation (P03); sympathetic fibres do the opposite. [T]

## 2 · Afferent supply and pain

**Atlas view.** [NEEDS ASSET]. Structures for orientation: `glisson-s-capsule`, `porta-hepatis`, `diaphragm`, `central-tendon-of-the-diaphragm`.

### 2.1 Where sensory fibres end
- Vagal and spinal afferents are restricted to the connective tissue around hepatic vessels and bile ducts and to the extrahepatic portal vein and bile ducts; sensory fibres are not found within the parenchyma. [V: Berthoud 2004, review]
- The capsule is also innervated. Together, this explains the pain of capsular stretch (acute hepatitis, congestion, haemorrhage into a tumour) and of ductal distension, and the absence of pain in parenchymal disease that does not distend the capsule. [T; V for the distribution]

### 2.2 Referred pain
- Fibres from the diaphragmatic surface and the central diaphragm travel with the phrenic nerve (C3–C5): pain from subphrenic or dome pathology is felt at the shoulder tip. [T; the atlas callout for the diaphragm makes the same point]
- Biliary pain travels with the right splanchnic/coeliac afferents and is felt in the epigastrium or right upper quadrant, sometimes at the right scapula. [T]

## 3 · Clinical correlation

### 3.1 Coeliac plexus neurolysis in pancreatic cancer
- The pancreas and upper abdominal viscera share the coeliac plexus afferents. In a double-blind randomised trial of newly diagnosed, painful, inoperable pancreatic adenocarcinoma, early endoscopic-ultrasound-guided coeliac plexus neurolysis reduced pain and may moderate morphine consumption compared with conventional pain management. [V: Wyse 2011; RCT, single trial]
- Current national guidance on pain control in pancreatic cancer: not reviewed here. [UNVERIFIED]

### 3.2 The denervated liver
- The transplanted liver is denervated and still functions, showing that autonomic control is modulatory, not essential to hepatic function. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**, no source verified]

## 4 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Where do sensory nerve fibres of the liver end?
A. Within the hepatocyte plates  B. In the connective tissue around vessels and ducts and in the capsule  C. Only in the central veins  D. Within the space of Disse  E. In the sinusoidal endothelium
**Answer: B.** Afferent fibres are restricted to the stroma around vessels and bile ducts and to the capsule. The parenchyma itself is not innervated.

**Q2.** Right shoulder-tip pain in a patient with a right subphrenic collection is most likely referred via:
A. The vagus nerve  B. The greater splanchnic nerve  C. The phrenic nerve  D. The intercostal nerves  E. The sympathetic chain at T12
**Answer: C.** The central diaphragm's sensory fibres travel in the phrenic nerve (C3–C5).

**Q3.** Which effect is correct for sympathetic stimulation of the liver?
A. Decreased gluconeogenesis  B. Increased hepatic glucose production  C. Gallbladder contraction  D. Increased bile flow through sphincter relaxation  E. Portal vasodilation
**Answer: B.** The sympathetic system stimulates and the parasympathetic suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis.

**Q4.** In a randomised trial of inoperable painful pancreatic cancer, early EUS-guided coeliac plexus neurolysis compared with conventional pain management:
A. Cured pain permanently  B. Reduced pain and may reduce morphine consumption  C. Increased morphine use  D. Had no effect  E. Improved survival by 12 months
**Answer: B.** Wyse 2011 reported pain reduction and possible morphine sparing; it was a single trial, so the evidence is limited.

**Q5.** Which is the best explanation for painless hepatitis in a patient with mild acute hepatocellular injury?
A. The liver has no nerves  B. Sensory fibres are restricted to the capsule and the tissue around vessels and ducts  C. Pain fibres are blocked by bile acids  D. The sympathetic supply is deficient  E. Pain is not carried by the vagus
**Answer: B.** Pain arises when the capsule or ducts are stretched, not from parenchymal cell injury alone.
