# P01 · Hepatic blood flow and its regulation

**Level:** MRCP  **Prerequisites:** A02, A03, A11  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook physiology (Guyton and Hall, Sherlock), not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Vessel anatomy is in A02/A03 and sinusoidal architecture in A11; neither is repeated.

## Learning outcomes
1. State the sources and approximate proportions of hepatic blood flow and oxygen delivery.
2. Explain the hepatic arterial buffer response, including its mediator and its limits.
3. Describe sinusoidal tone and how it is regulated.
4. Predict the flow and pressure changes after a meal, in shock, and after portal vein occlusion.

## 1 · Inflow and oxygen

### 1.1 Proportions
- The liver receives about a quarter of cardiac output. The portal vein supplies roughly three-quarters of the flow at low pressure and partly deoxygenated blood; the hepatic artery supplies the rest at systemic pressure. The two supply oxygen in broadly similar amounts. [T: Guyton and Hall; exact percentages **[UNVERIFIED]**]
- Arterial blood perfuses the biliary plexus and the portal tract structures (A04); both inflows converge on the sinusoids. [T]

### 1.2 Pressure
- Portal pressure is low (a few mmHg above hepatic venous pressure); the sinusoidal bed is low-resistance, which is why the gradient between wedged and free hepatic venous pressure (HVPG) is small in health. [T; HVPG in P07]

## 2 · The hepatic arterial buffer response

**Atlas view.** Tier 0. `portal` On, `artery` On, tier 1 `flow`. Pathology state: portal vein thrombosis (`state.path.pvt`) to show the arterial side responding.
Buttons: **"Occlude the portal vein"** (sets PVT; highlight `portal-vein-thrombus-acute-occlusive`) · **"Reset"**.

### 2.1 Mechanism
- When portal inflow falls, hepatic arterial flow rises. The adenosine washout hypothesis: adenosine is released at a constant rate into the space of Mall around the arterial resistance vessels and portal venules, and is washed away by portal flow; when portal flow falls, adenosine accumulates and dilates the artery. [V: Lautt 1985]
- The response can buffer 25–60% of the fall in portal flow. [V: Eipel 2010, review]

### 2.2 Limits
- It is one-way: reduced arterial flow does not cause a compensatory portal increase. [V: Eipel 2010; T]
- Buffering is partial, so ischaemia can still result from combined insults. [T]

## 3 · Sinusoidal tone and autoregulation

### 3.1 Stellate cells and endothelium
- Stellate cells (A11) and sinusoidal endothelium regulate sinusoidal calibre through nitric oxide (relaxation) and endothelin-1 (contraction). [T; V for the concept that both structural and vasoactive components raise hepatic resistance: Bosch 2015]
- In cirrhosis, the balance shifts towards increased intrahepatic vascular tone; this is the reversible component of portal hypertension (P07). [V: Bosch 2015, review]

### 3.2 Physiological variation
- Flow rises after a meal (splanchnic hyperaemia) and falls with upright posture, exercise and sympathetic activation. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**, no source verified]

## 4 · Clinical correlation

### 4.1 Hypoxic (ischaemic) hepatitis
- Cardiac failure, respiratory failure and septic shock account for more than 90% of cases; the lesion is centrilobular (zone 3) necrosis with a rapid, massive aminotransferase rise. [V: Henrion 2012; A11]
- The buffer response fails when arterial flow is also low (shock), so the lowest-oxygen zone infarcts. [T]

### 4.2 Portal vein thrombosis
- Acute PVT is often tolerated because the artery dilates; chronic PVT develops collaterals (`portal-cavernoma`). [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]
- Guidance: EASL vascular liver disease CPG 2025 supersedes 2016; Baveno VIII (2026) supersedes Baveno VII. [V: existence]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** What is the accepted mediator of the hepatic arterial buffer response?
A. Nitric oxide  B. Adenosine  C. Endothelin-1  D. Noradrenaline  E. Angiotensin II
**Answer: B.** Adenosine accumulates in the space of Mall when portal flow falls (Lautt 1985).

**Q2.** Portal venous flow falls by 50%. The hepatic arterial buffer response would:
A. Reduce arterial flow  B. Increase arterial flow, buffering part of the fall  C. Have no effect  D. Fully restore total flow  E. Increase portal flow
**Answer: B.** It can buffer 25–60% of the fall in portal flow; it does not fully restore it.

**Q3.** Which change is characteristic of intrahepatic vascular tone in cirrhosis?
A. Reduced tone  B. Increased tone, partly reversible  C. No change  D. Increased NO only  E. Reduced endothelin
**Answer: B.** Increased vasoactive tone adds to structural resistance (Bosch 2015).

**Q4.** Which cause accounts for most cases of hypoxic hepatitis?
A. Drugs  B. Viral hepatitis  C. Cardiac failure, respiratory failure or septic shock  D. Biliary obstruction  E. Autoimmune disease
**Answer: C.** Over 90% (Henrion 2012).

**Q5.** The hepatic arterial buffer response works in which direction?
A. Portal flow compensates for arterial flow  B. Arterial flow compensates for reduced portal flow  C. Both directions  D. Neither  E. Only during exercise
**Answer: B.** It is one-directional.
