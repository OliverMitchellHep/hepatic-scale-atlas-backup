# P04 · Hepatic metabolism: carbohydrate, lipid, protein and ammonia

**Level:** MRCP  **Prerequisites:** P02  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook physiology/biochemistry, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Zonal distribution of pathways is in P02 and is not repeated.

## Learning outcomes
1. Describe the fed and fasted switch in hepatic glucose and lipid handling and its hormonal control.
2. Explain VLDL assembly, β-oxidation and ketogenesis.
3. Trace ammonia through the urea cycle and glutamine synthesis and say what fails in liver disease.
4. Relate the metabolic pathways to MASLD and hepatic encephalopathy.

## 1 · Carbohydrate

**Atlas view.** Tier 4 (hepatocyte). `glycogen` On, `mitochondria` On, `er` On, `nucleus` Ghost.
Buttons: **"Show glycogen"** (highlight `glycogen`) · **"Show mitochondria"** (`mitochondria`) · **"Show ER"** (`endoplasmic-reticulum`).

### 1.1 Fed and fasted states
- Fed: glucose is converted to pyruvate by glycolysis, and glycolytic products are used for de novo lipogenesis; insulin stimulates glycolysis and lipogenesis and suppresses gluconeogenesis. [V: Rui 2014, review]
- Fasted: the liver releases glucose through glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis; in prolonged fasting gluconeogenesis is the main source of endogenous glucose. Glucagon counteracts insulin. [V: Rui 2014]
- Glycogen stores are limited, which is why acute liver failure and advanced cirrhosis cause fasting hypoglycaemia. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

**Atlas view.** Tier 4. `lipid` On, `mitochondria` On. Pathology: MASLD slider (`state.path.masld`, `mash`).
Buttons: **"Show lipid droplets"** (highlight `lipid-droplets`) · **"Show macrovesicular fat"** (`macrovesicular-fat-droplet`, `fat-droplets-macrovesicular-steatosis`) · **"Switch on MASLD"**.

### 2.1 Pathways
- Fed state: long-chain fatty acids are incorporated into triacylglycerol, phospholipids and cholesterol esters, and exported as VLDL. [V: Rui 2014]
- Fasted state: fatty acids are β-oxidised, and ketone bodies made in mitochondria fuel extrahepatic tissues. [V: Rui 2014]

### 2.2 MASLD
- MASLD is defined as hepatic steatosis with at least one cardiometabolic risk factor and no other discernible cause. [V: EASL–EASD–EASO 2024, J Hepatol 81:492–542]
- Steatosis reflects excess delivery and synthesis of lipid relative to oxidation and export; insulin resistance drives both adipose lipolysis and hepatic lipogenesis. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 3 · Protein and ammonia

**Atlas view.** Tier 4 `mitochondria`, `er`, `ribosome`. Perivenous glutamine synthesis: [NEEDS ASSET].

### 3.1 Urea cycle
- The liver converts ammonia to urea (periportal, high-capacity); urea is excreted by the kidneys. When capacity is exceeded, hyperammonaemia results. [V: AASLD/EASL HE guideline 2014, search summary; cycle steps T]
- Perivenous glutamine synthetase scavenges residual ammonia (P02). [T]

### 3.2 Hepatic encephalopathy
- Ammonia is central but not sufficient. The 2014 AASLD/EASL guideline states that a raised blood ammonia alone adds no diagnostic, staging or prognostic value in chronic liver disease, and recommends lactulose as the treatment of choice for overt HE and for secondary prophylaxis. [V: Vilstrup 2014, search summary; **guideline is over 5 years old, check for an update**]
- Portosystemic shunts (A09) bypass hepatic clearance. [T]

## 4 · Clinical correlation

### 4.1 MASLD and the liver's metabolic switch
- Hepatic insulin resistance leaves gluconeogenesis unsuppressed while lipogenesis remains active, so hyperglycaemia and steatosis coexist. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]
- Current guidance: EASL–EASD–EASO 2024 (within 5 years). [V]

### 4.2 Hypoglycaemia in liver failure
- Loss of glycogen stores and gluconeogenic capacity causes hypoglycaemia in acute liver failure. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which hormone suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis?
A. Glucagon  B. Insulin  C. Adrenaline  D. Cortisol  E. Growth hormone
**Answer: B.**

**Q2.** During prolonged fasting, the main source of hepatic glucose output is:
A. Glycogenolysis  B. Gluconeogenesis  C. Glycolysis  D. Lipogenesis  E. Ketogenesis
**Answer: B.** (Rui 2014.)

**Q3.** The 2014 AASLD/EASL HE guideline says a raised blood ammonia in chronic liver disease:
A. Is required for diagnosis  B. Adds no diagnostic, staging or prognostic value  C. Predicts mortality  D. Guides lactulose dosing  E. Should be repeated daily
**Answer: B.** Guideline is over 5 years old.

**Q4.** MASLD is defined as steatosis with:
A. No other criteria  B. At least one cardiometabolic risk factor and no other discernible cause  C. Alcohol intake above threshold  D. Fibrosis stage 2 or more  E. Raised ALT
**Answer: B.**

**Q5.** Which is the treatment of choice for overt HE in the 2014 guideline?
A. Rifaximin alone  B. Lactulose  C. Protein restriction  D. Neomycin  E. Flumazenil
**Answer: B.** Lactulose, for treatment and secondary prevention. **[V: search summary; check the guideline text and any newer guidance.]**
