# P07 · Portal pressure physiology

**Level:** MRCP → registrar  **Prerequisites:** P01, A09  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook physiology, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Collateral routes are in A09 and the resection thresholds in A02; neither is repeated.

## Learning outcomes
1. Express portal pressure as flow × resistance and identify the components that raise each.
2. Define HVPG, what it measures and where it fails, and classify portal hypertension as pre-, sinusoidal or post-sinusoidal.
3. Explain the splanchnic vasodilatation and hyperdynamic circulation of cirrhosis.
4. State the clinical thresholds and the logic of β-blockers and TIPS.

## 1 · Pressure, flow and resistance

### 1.1 The model
- Portal pressure = portal blood flow × resistance. Resistance rises in cirrhosis through structural distortion (fibrosis, nodules) and a dynamic, reversible increase in intrahepatic vascular tone; flow rises through splanchnic vasodilatation. [V: Bosch 2015, review]

### 1.2 Classification by site
- **Pre-sinusoidal** (portal vein thrombosis, schistosomiasis), **sinusoidal** (cirrhosis) and **post-sinusoidal** (hepatic vein or IVC obstruction, right heart failure). [T]
- The atlas includes PVT, Budd–Chiari and TIPS states to show each pattern. [Atlas]

## 2 · HVPG

**Atlas view.** HVPG dialog. Structures: `right-hepatic-vein`, `ivc`.
Buttons: **"Free hepatic venous pressure"** · **"Wedged hepatic venous pressure"** · **"HVPG = WHVP − FHVP"** (all steps in the atlas dialog).

### 2.1 Measurement
- The catheter is advanced into a hepatic vein; the balloon is inflated to occlude it, and the wedged pressure approximates sinusoidal pressure. HVPG is wedged minus free pressure. [T; the atlas dialog describes this]
- WHVP approximates portal pressure in sinusoidal disease, not in pre-sinusoidal disease, where sinusoids are normal and HVPG underestimates portal pressure. [T]

### 2.2 Thresholds
- Clinically significant portal hypertension is HVPG ≥10 mmHg. [V: Baveno VII 2022; **Baveno VIII (2026) supersedes**, not rechecked]
- In a randomised timolol trial, varices developed less frequently in patients with baseline HVPG below 10 mmHg, and non-selective β-blockade did not prevent varices in that population. [V: Groszmann 2005, NEJM trial, n=213]

## 3 · The hyperdynamic circulation

### 3.1 Mechanism
- The peripheral arterial vasodilation hypothesis proposes that splanchnic and systemic arterial vasodilation reduces effective arterial blood volume, activating renin–angiotensin–aldosterone and sympathetic systems and driving renal sodium and water retention. [V: Schrier 1988]
- The result is raised cardiac output, low systemic vascular resistance and increased splanchnic inflow, which maintain and worsen portal hypertension. [T; the concept V: Bosch 2015]

### 3.2 Collaterals
- Collaterals form under pressure and by angiogenesis but do not fully decompress the portal system; the collateral flow bypasses the liver (A09). [V: Bosch 2015 for angiogenesis; T]

## 4 · Clinical correlation

### 4.1 Drugs
- Non-selective β-blockers reduce portal pressure by reducing cardiac output and splanchnic flow. [T; guidance on use is in Baveno, **[UNVERIFIED]**]

### 4.2 TIPS
- A TIPS creates a low-resistance channel between portal and hepatic veins, lowering portal pressure but increasing hepatic encephalopathy risk through shunting. [T; the atlas models `tipss-stent` and related structures]

### 4.3 Patterns and imaging
- PVT: HVPG normal or minimally raised despite portal hypertension (pre-sinusoidal). Budd–Chiari: post-sinusoidal, with caudate enlargement (A02). [T]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Portal pressure is determined by:
A. Resistance only  B. Flow only  C. Flow multiplied by resistance  D. Portal vein diameter  E. Splenic size
**Answer: C.**

**Q2.** HVPG is:
A. Wedged minus free hepatic venous pressure  B. Portal minus IVC pressure  C. Free minus wedged  D. Arterial minus portal  E. Hepatic artery pressure
**Answer: A.**

**Q3.** In portal vein thrombosis without cirrhosis, HVPG is typically:
A. Markedly elevated  B. Normal or minimally raised  C. Negative  D. Equal to portal pressure  E. Unmeasurable
**Answer: B.** Sinusoids are normal, so the wedged pressure does not reflect the raised portal pressure. **[T]**

**Q4.** The peripheral arterial vasodilation hypothesis explains:
A. Portal vein thrombosis  B. Renal sodium and water retention in cirrhosis  C. Hepatic encephalopathy  D. Bile acid malabsorption  E. Varices only
**Answer: B.** (Schrier 1988.)

**Q5.** Clinically significant portal hypertension is defined by an HVPG of:
A. ≥5 mmHg  B. ≥10 mmHg  C. ≥12 mmHg  D. ≥16 mmHg  E. ≥20 mmHg
**Answer: B.** Baveno VII; check against Baveno VIII.
