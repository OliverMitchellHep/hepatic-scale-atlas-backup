# A01 · Liver: gross structure, surfaces and peritoneal attachments

**Level:** student  **Prerequisite:** none  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook anatomy (Gray's, Netter, Blumgart, Sherlock), not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Segmental anatomy is in A02 and is not repeated here.

## Learning outcomes
1. Describe the liver's position, surfaces and margins, and its relations to the diaphragm, ribs and adjacent organs.
2. Name each peritoneal attachment, say what it contains or separates, and locate the bare area.
3. Identify the contents and arrangement of the porta hepatis and the structures that mark the IVC groove and gallbladder fossa.
4. Relate surface anatomy to percutaneous biopsy, palpation and referred pain.

## 1 · Position, surfaces and margins

### 1.1 Position
- The liver lies mostly in the right upper quadrant under the diaphragm, protected by the lower right ribs, and extends across the midline into the left upper quadrant. [T]
- The right dome sits higher than the left. In the CT-derived atlas model the difference is small because the scan is supine and inspiratory. [Atlas note]

### 1.2 Surfaces
- **Diaphragmatic surface:** smooth, domed, anterior, superior and right lateral; the falciform ligament attaches to it. [T]
- **Visceral surface:** faces inferiorly and posteriorly, moulded by adjacent organs: the stomach and duodenum, the gallbladder, the right kidney and adrenal, the hepatic flexure of the colon and the oesophagus. It carries the H-shaped arrangement of fissures and fossae (Section 3). [T]

### 1.3 Capsule
- Glisson's capsule is a thin fibrous layer over the peritoneum-covered surface; it thickens along the portal pedicles as the Glisson sheath. [T]
- Sensory nerves supply the capsule and the connective tissue around vessels and bile ducts, not the parenchyma (Berthoud 2004, review of sensory hepatic nerves). [V]
- Rapid liver enlargement stretches the capsule and produces right upper quadrant pain; hepatic parenchymal disease alone is painless. [T]

## 2 · Peritoneal attachments

**Atlas view.** Tier 0. `ligaments` On, `parenchyma` Solid, `diaphragm` Ghost.
Buttons: **"Falciform and round ligaments"** (highlight `falciform-ligament`, `ligamentum-teres`) · **"Coronary ligament"** (`coronary-ligament`) · **"Bare area"** [NEEDS ASSET] · **"Lesser omentum"** [NEEDS ASSET] · **"Triangular ligaments"** [NEEDS ASSET].
Note: the atlas paints the falciform into the liver texture; triangular ligaments are not modelled and the ligamentum teres is hidden in the scene.

### 2.1 Anterior
- **Falciform ligament:** a double peritoneal fold from the anterior abdominal wall and diaphragm to the liver's anterior-superior surface. Its free edge contains the **ligamentum teres**, the obliterated left umbilical vein, which runs to the umbilical notch and joins the left portal vein at the umbilical portion. [T]
- The falciform ligament marks the anatomical division between the left lateral section and segment IV (A02, section 1.2).

### 2.2 Superior and posterior
- **Coronary ligament:** the upper and lower peritoneal reflections between the liver and the diaphragm. They enclose the **bare area**, a region of the posterior-superior surface in direct contact with the diaphragm, without peritoneal covering. [T]
- **Triangular ligaments:** the right and left lateral extensions of the coronary ligament. [T]
- The bare area is why a right-sided subphrenic or retroperitoneal process can involve the liver surface without entering the peritoneal cavity. [T]

### 2.3 Inferior
- **Lesser omentum** (hepatogastric and hepatoduodenal ligaments) runs from the porta hepatis and the fissure for the ligamentum venosum to the lesser curve of the stomach and the first part of the duodenum. Its free edge, the hepatoduodenal ligament, carries the portal triad. [T]
- **Ligamentum venosum:** the obliterated ductus venosus, in a fissure on the visceral surface, lying between segment I and segment II. [T]

## 3 · Visceral surface, porta hepatis and lobes

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, inferior view. `parenchyma` Ghost, `portal` On, `artery` On, `biliary` On, `hepaticVein` On.
Buttons: **"Porta hepatis"** (highlight `porta-hepatis`, `portal-trunk`, `proper-hepatic-artery`, `common-hepatic-duct`) · **"IVC groove"** (`ivc`, `caudate-lobe`) · **"Gallbladder fossa"** (`gallbladder`).

### 3.1 The "H" pattern
- The visceral surface is organised by two sagittal fossae joined by the transverse porta hepatis. Right: the gallbladder fossa (anteriorly) and the IVC groove (posteriorly). Left: the fissure for the ligamentum teres (anteriorly) and for the ligamentum venosum (posteriorly). [T]
- This produces four visceral "lobes": right, left, quadrate (anterior to the porta, between the gallbladder fossa and the umbilical fissure) and caudate (posterior). The quadrate lobe is segment IVb. [T]

### 3.2 Porta hepatis
- The portal vein lies posteriorly. The hepatic artery lies anterior and to the left of it, and the hepatic duct anterior and to the right. [T]
- The right and left ducts, arteries and portal branches divide within or just outside the hilum; their extrahepatic segments are in A03 and A04.

### 3.3 Lobes
- Anatomical lobes (right and left, split at the falciform ligament) and functional hemilivers (split at Cantlie's line) are different divisions; the functional one is the one that matters for surgery and is covered in A02. [T; see A02]

## 4 · Clinical correlation

### 4.1 Where to biopsy
- Percutaneous liver biopsy is usually taken through an intercostal space in the right mid-axillary line, aiming for the right lobe; the atlas biopsy simulation shows this route against right ribs 7–12. [T; atlas]
- BSG/RCR/RCPath guidance (2020): obtain the biopsy under ultrasound guidance where possible, or image the liver within 3 months to plan the site; use a 16 G needle for the percutaneous route, with 18 G in certain circumstances. [V: Neuberger 2020. Guideline is over 5 years old at the time of writing; check for an update.] Grades not read: [UNVERIFIED].

### 4.2 Palpable and referred findings
- A tongue-like inferior projection of the right lobe (Riedel lobe) is a normal variant that may present as a palpable right hypochondrial mass and is usually incidental on imaging. [V: case-report literature only; prevalence figures in the literature vary widely and are not given here.]
- Irritation of the central diaphragm can be referred to the shoulder tip via the phrenic nerve, which is why subphrenic or hepatic dome pathology can cause right shoulder-tip pain (A06). [T]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which structure lies within the free edge of the falciform ligament?
A. Ligamentum venosum  B. Ligamentum teres  C. Cystic duct  D. Left hepatic vein  E. Hepatic artery
**Answer: B.** The ligamentum teres is the obliterated left umbilical vein and runs in the falciform ligament's free edge to the umbilical notch. The ligamentum venosum lies in a separate posterior fissure.

**Q2.** The bare area of the liver is bounded by:
A. The falciform ligament  B. The coronary ligament  C. The lesser omentum  D. The ligamentum venosum  E. The gallbladder fossa
**Answer: B.** The upper and lower layers of the coronary ligament enclose the area where the liver contacts the diaphragm without peritoneum.

**Q3.** In the porta hepatis, which is the most posterior structure?
A. Common hepatic duct  B. Hepatic artery  C. Portal vein  D. Cystic duct  E. Left hepatic duct
**Answer: C.** The portal vein lies posterior, with the hepatic artery anterior-left and the duct anterior-right.

**Q4.** The quadrate lobe corresponds to which Couinaud segment?
A. I  B. II  C. III  D. IVb  E. V
**Answer: D.** The quadrate lobe is segment IVb; the caudate lobe is segment I.

**Q5.** Which is the best supported statement about percutaneous liver biopsy according to BSG/RCR/RCPath 2020 guidance?
A. Blind biopsy is preferred  B. Ultrasound guidance should be used where possible  C. A 22 G needle is standard  D. A left-lobe approach is preferred  E. Imaging is unnecessary if the patient has cirrhosis
**Answer: B.** The guideline recommends ultrasound guidance where possible, or recent imaging to plan the site, and a 16 G needle. [Guideline 2020, more than 5 years old; grade not read, **[UNVERIFIED]**.]
