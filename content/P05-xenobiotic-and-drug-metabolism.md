**Level:** MRCP → registrar  **Prerequisites:** P02  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook pharmacology/physiology, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Extraction ratio and first-pass metabolism are defined in P02 and are not repeated.

## Learning outcomes
1. Describe phase I, II and III metabolism and where each occurs in the hepatocyte.
2. Explain why cytochrome P450 variability matters and why zone 3 is exposed to reactive metabolites.
3. Trace paracetamol metabolism, NAPQI formation and glutathione depletion, and explain the rationale for acetylcysteine.
4. Apply this to drug dosing in cirrhosis and to interactions.

### 1.1 Phases
- **Phase I** (oxidation, mainly cytochrome P450 in the smooth ER), **phase II** (conjugation: glucuronidation, sulfation, glutathione), **phase III** (export by transporters into bile or blood, P03). [T]
- P450 enzymes show large interindividual variability from genetics and regulation; this drives differences in drug exposure. [V: Zanger & Schwab 2013, review]

### 1.2 Zonation
- CYP enzymes are concentrated in perivenous hepatocytes (A11, P02), which is why zone 3 receives the largest dose of reactive metabolites. [T; A11]

## 2 · Paracetamol

**Atlas view.** Tier 1–3. Pathology: zonal injury `apap` (`state.path.zonal`).
Buttons: **"Paracetamol injury"** (sets `zonal` = `apap`) · **"Clear"**.

### 2.1 Metabolism
- At therapeutic doses paracetamol is mainly conjugated (glucuronide, sulfate). A small fraction is oxidised by CYP enzymes to the reactive metabolite NAPQI, which glutathione detoxifies. In overdose the conjugation pathways saturate, glutathione is exhausted and NAPQI binds cellular proteins, causing centrilobular (zone 3) necrosis. [T; the atlas ZON_NOTE makes the same point]

### 2.2 Treatment
- N-acetylcysteine restores glutathione; EASL ALF 2017 recommends it in all paracetamol overdoses. [V: EASL 2017 via search summary; guideline is over 5 years old, check for an update]
- UK practice since September 2012: a single treatment line starting at 100 mg/L at 4 hours for all patients with acute overdose; treat all staggered or uncertain-timing ingestions irrespective of concentration; the initial infusion lengthened from 15 to 60 minutes. [V: search summaries of the Commission on Human Medicines changes; guidance may have changed since (for example the 12-hour SNAP regimen), so **[UNVERIFIED currency]**; check TOXBASE/NICE before using in teaching]

## 3 · Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) and dosing

### 3.1 DILI
- Patterns are hepatocellular, cholestatic or mixed. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]
- Guidelines: EASL DILI CPG 2019 and other guidance not searched here. [UNVERIFIED]

### 3.2 Dosing in cirrhosis
- Reduced enzyme activity, capillarisation and shunting alter clearance; hepatic dosing adjustment is drug-specific and should follow the product information. [T; V for capillarisation (DeLeve 2015)]

## 4 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which reactive metabolite causes paracetamol hepatotoxicity?
A. Glucuronide  B. Sulfate  C. NAPQI  D. Glutathione  E. Cysteine
**Answer: C.** NAPQI, made by CYP oxidation, is detoxified by glutathione.

**Q2.** Paracetamol-induced necrosis begins in:
A. Zone 1  B. Zone 2  C. Zone 3  D. The portal tract  E. The limiting plate
**Answer: C.** Zone 3 is enriched for CYP enzymes.

**Q3.** Acetylcysteine acts by:
A. Inhibiting CYP2E1 only  B. Restoring glutathione  C. Increasing glucuronidation only  D. Chelating paracetamol  E. Increasing bile flow
**Answer: B.**

**Q4.** Under the 2012 UK guidance as summarised, which patients are treated irrespective of paracetamol concentration?
A. Patients with a single acute overdose at 4 hours  B. Those with staggered or uncertain-timing ingestions  C. Those with risk factors only  D. Children only  E. None
**Answer: B.** **[UNVERIFIED currency; check current UK guidance.]**

**Q5.** Which explains variable drug exposure between individuals?
A. Uniform CYP expression  B. Genetic and regulatory variation in P450 enzymes  C. Body weight only  D. Gastric pH only  E. Bile acid level
**Answer: B.** (Zanger & Schwab 2013.)
