# A03 · Extrahepatic vasculature: coeliac axis, splanchnic veins and hepatoduodenal ligament

**Level:** student → MRCP  **Prerequisites:** A01, A02  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook anatomy, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Intrahepatic portal, arterial and venous anatomy, and the hepatic arterial variants, are in A02. Portosystemic collaterals are in A09.

## Learning outcomes
1. Trace the arterial supply from the aorta to the liver, stomach, pancreas and spleen, and name the collateral routes between the coeliac and superior mesenteric territories.
2. Describe the formation of the portal vein and the venous tributaries that drain to it, with the variable insertion of the inferior mesenteric vein.
3. State the arrangement of structures in the hepatoduodenal ligament and their relations to the epiploic foramen.
4. Use this anatomy to plan or interpret arterial embolisation, hepatic artery thrombosis and portal vein thrombosis.

## 1 · Coeliac axis and hepatic arterial supply

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior. `artery` On, `parenchyma` Ghost, `stomach` Ghost, `pancreas` Ghost, `spleen` Ghost.
Buttons: **"Coeliac trunk and its three branches"** (highlight `coeliac-trunk`, `left-gastric-artery`, `splenic-artery`, `common-hepatic-artery`) · **"Common hepatic to proper hepatic"** (`common-hepatic-artery`, `gastroduodenal-artery`, `proper-hepatic-artery`) · **"Superior mesenteric artery"** (`superior-mesenteric-artery`, `aorta`).

### 1.1 Origin and branches
- The coeliac trunk arises from the aorta at about T12 and divides into the left gastric, splenic and common hepatic arteries. [T]
- The common hepatic artery gives the gastroduodenal artery (GDA) and continues as the proper hepatic artery; the atlas models the CHA running along the upper border of the pancreas, in front of and above the portal trunk. [T; atlas note, v46]
- Michels' 1966 dissection series (200 specimens) established ten basic patterns of hepatic supply and 26 collateral routes. [V: Michels 1966] Prevalence of the variants is in A02, section 4.2 and A10.

### 1.2 Gastroduodenal artery and pancreaticoduodenal arcades
- The GDA passes behind the first part of the duodenum, descends on the anterior surface of the pancreatic head and divides into the right gastroepiploic and superior pancreaticoduodenal arteries. [T; atlas models this]
- The pancreaticoduodenal arcades join the coeliac and superior mesenteric territories. This is the main collateral route if either the coeliac trunk or the SMA is stenosed. [T]

### 1.3 Cystic artery
- Usually arises from the right hepatic artery, in the cystohepatic triangle. Its origin varies. [T; prevalence figures in the literature are inconsistent and are not given here.]

## 2 · The portal vein and its tributaries

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior. `portal` On, `artery` Ghost, `parenchyma` Ghost, `spleen` On, `stomach` Ghost, `pancreas` On.
Buttons: **"Form the portal vein"** (highlight `superior-mesenteric-vein`, `splenic-vein`, `portal-trunk`) · **"Show tributaries"** (`inferior-mesenteric-vein`, `left-gastric-vein`) · **"Ghost the pancreas"** (`pancreas` Ghost).

### 2.1 Formation
- The portal trunk forms behind the neck of the pancreas from the superior mesenteric and splenic veins. [T]
- The left gastric (coronary) vein drains the lesser curve and lower oesophagus into the portal trunk or splenic vein. [T]

### 2.2 Inferior mesenteric vein
- The IMV most often drains into the splenic vein; it may drain instead into the confluence or the SMV. In one large autopsy series it joined the distal splenic vein in 38.0%, the confluence in 32.7% and the SMV in 29.3%. [V: figures from a search summary; source authors not identified, so **[UNVERIFIED citation]**; another small specimen series gave 73% splenic vein]
- Practical point: the drainage site affects what a splenic-vein-only stent or a pancreatic resection leaves in continuity. [T]

### 2.3 Cystic and paraumbilical veins
- The cystic vein drains directly to the portal branch of the right lobe. Small paraumbilical veins run in the falciform ligament to the left portal vein and become collaterals in portal hypertension (A09). [T]

## 3 · The hepatoduodenal ligament

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior. `portal` On, `artery` On, `biliary` On, `parenchyma` Ghost. [NEEDS ASSET: hepatoduodenal ligament and epiploic foramen].
Buttons: **"Portal triad"** (highlight `portal-trunk`, `proper-hepatic-artery`, `common-bile-duct`) · **"Show the epiploic foramen"** [NEEDS ASSET].

### 3.1 Contents and arrangement
- Free edge of the lesser omentum, from the porta hepatis to the first part of the duodenum. It contains the bile duct (right, anterior), the hepatic artery (left, anterior) and the portal vein (posterior), with lymphatics, nerves and lymph nodes. [T]

### 3.2 Epiploic foramen
- The epiploic foramen (of Winslow) lies behind the free edge, with the IVC posterior and the caudate process superior. Placing a finger in the foramen and compressing the ligament between thumb and finger is the Pringle manoeuvre, which interrupts inflow. [T; V: Pringle 1908 for the manoeuvre]

## 4 · Clinical correlation

### 4.1 Arterial mapping before embolisation
- Transarterial chemo- and radioembolisation deliver particles through the hepatic artery; non-target embolisation can occur through the cystic, pancreaticoduodenal, right gastric or falciform arteries and the GDA, and has been reported to cause cholecystitis, pancreatitis, gastroduodenal injury or skin necrosis. [V: review-level sources found by search; author details not verified, **[UNVERIFIED]**]
- This is why pretreatment CT angiography maps hepatic arterial variants, the GDA, the falciform artery (which supplies the anterior abdominal wall) and the cystic artery. [V: same sources; T]

### 4.2 Portal vein thrombosis
- PVT interrupts portal inflow and drives the formation of a collateral network around the thrombosed vein (portal cavernoma), which the atlas models (`portal-cavernoma`, `portal-vein-thrombus-*`). [T]
- Current guidance: EASL vascular liver disease CPG **2025** supersedes 2016; Baveno **VIII (2026)** supersedes Baveno VII on PVT and Budd–Chiari management. [V: existence and dates; content not read]

### 4.3 Arterial supply after hepatic artery loss
- The liver tolerates loss of the hepatic artery poorly in the transplant setting because the biliary tree depends on arterial supply; extrahepatic collaterals through the pancreaticoduodenal arcades may partly rescue perfusion in other settings. [T; no source verified for this statement, **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which artery arises directly from the common hepatic artery and descends on the anterior surface of the pancreatic head?
A. Left gastric  B. Splenic  C. Gastroduodenal  D. Cystic  E. Right gastric
**Answer: C.** The GDA passes behind D1 and descends on the pancreatic head, dividing into the right gastroepiploic and superior pancreaticoduodenal arteries.

**Q2.** Which vessels form the portal vein?
A. Superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric veins  B. Splenic and inferior mesenteric veins  C. Superior mesenteric and splenic veins  D. Hepatic and splenic veins  E. Left and right gastric veins
**Answer: C.** The IMV joins the splenic vein, the confluence or the SMV, but is not one of the two formative veins.

**Q3.** In the hepatoduodenal ligament, the portal vein lies:
A. Anterior to the artery and duct  B. Posterior to the artery and duct  C. To the right of the duct  D. Lateral to the epiploic foramen  E. Within the free edge, anterior to the duct
**Answer: B.** The bile duct is anterior-right, the artery anterior-left, the portal vein posterior.

**Q4.** What is the principal collateral route between the coeliac and superior mesenteric arterial territories?
A. Left gastric and splenic arteries  B. Pancreaticoduodenal arcades  C. Cystic artery  D. Inferior phrenic arteries  E. Middle colic artery
**Answer: B.** The arcades link the GDA (coeliac) with the inferior pancreaticoduodenal artery (SMA).

**Q5.** A radiologist performing TACE notes a small vessel arising from the left hepatic artery running to the anterior abdominal wall. The main risk of non-target embolisation of this vessel is:
A. Pancreatitis  B. Cholecystitis  C. Skin necrosis  D. Gastric ulcer  E. Splenic infarction
**Answer: C.** The hepatic falciform artery supplies the anterior abdominal wall. Non-target embolisation has been reported to cause skin injury. The other complications relate to the GDA, cystic and right gastric arteries.
