# P09 · Splanchnic circulation, ascites and the hepatorenal axis

**Level:** registrar  **Prerequisites:** P07, A07  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

## Learning outcomes
1. Explain ascites formation from Starling forces, lymph overflow and renal sodium retention.
2. Describe the neurohumoral response and the transition from sodium retention to HRS-AKI.
3. State the ICA–ADQI HRS-AKI criteria.
4. Link physiology to diuretics, albumin and paracentesis.

Buttons: **"Show the renal veins and kidneys"** (highlight `right-kidney`, `left-kidney`, `renal-artery`, `renal-vein`) · **"Raise portal pressure"** (fibrosis slider).

### 1.1 Forces
- In sinusoidal portal hypertension, raised hydrostatic pressure in the sinusoids and splanchnic capillaries, with lowered oncotic pressure from hypoalbuminaemia, increases lymph formation; the lymphatic capacity is exceeded and fluid accumulates in the peritoneal cavity (A07). [T]
- Renal sodium and water retention perpetuates the process. [V: Schrier 1988; P07]

### 1.2 Neurohumoral activation
- Splanchnic vasodilation lowers effective arterial volume and activates the RAAS, sympathetic nervous system and vasopressin. [V: Schrier 1988; T]

## 2 · The hepatorenal axis

**Atlas view.** Tier 0. `kidneys` On, `portal` On.

### 2.1 HRS-AKI
- The ADQI/ICA joint consensus (2024) revised HRS-AKI criteria: cirrhosis with ascites and an increase in serum creatinine of at least 0.3 mg/dL (26.5 µmol/L) within 48 hours or at least 50% from baseline. [V: Nadim 2024, from search summary; other criteria (absence of shock, nephrotoxins, structural disease) T, **[UNVERIFIED]**]
- HRS is a functional AKI from extreme renal vasoconstriction in advanced cirrhosis. [T]

### 2.2 Management logic
- Diuretics (spironolactone, loop) treat sodium retention; large-volume paracentesis is accompanied by albumin. [T; guideline EASL 2018, over 5 years old, **[UNVERIFIED]** for the wording]
- Vasoconstrictors plus albumin are used in HRS-AKI. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**; check ADQI/ICA 2024 text]

## 3 · Clinical correlation

- **Diuretic-resistant ascites and refractory ascites:** consider TIPS and transplant assessment. [T]
- **SBP:** infected ascites precipitates HRS-AKI; albumin is given. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]
- **Long-term albumin:** ANSWER open-label trial (P06). [V: Caraceni 2018]

## 4 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** The peripheral arterial vasodilation hypothesis proposes that sodium retention in cirrhosis begins with:
A. Primary renal failure  B. Splanchnic and systemic arterial vasodilation  C. Hypoalbuminaemia alone  D. Cardiac failure  E. Portal vein thrombosis
**Answer: B.**

**Q2.** Which is part of the 2024 ICA–ADQI HRS-AKI criteria as summarised?
A. Creatinine rise ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours or ≥50% from baseline in a patient with cirrhosis and ascites  B. Urine sodium >100 mmol/L  C. Proteinuria >500 mg/day  D. Renal size <9 cm  E. Serum sodium <120
**Answer: A.**

**Q3.** Large-volume paracentesis should be accompanied by:
A. Terlipressin  B. Albumin  C. Furosemide  D. Octreotide  E. Antibiotics
**Answer: B.** [T; EASL 2018.]

**Q4.** Ascites forms mainly because of:
A. Portal vein thrombosis  B. Raised sinusoidal pressure with lymph overflow and renal sodium retention  C. Hepatic vein dilation  D. Reduced bile flow  E. Splenic enlargement
**Answer: B.**

**Q5.** Which is a neurohumoral response in advanced cirrhosis?
A. RAAS activation  B. Suppressed vasopressin  C. Suppressed sympathetic tone  D. Increased ANP only  E. No response
**Answer: A.**
