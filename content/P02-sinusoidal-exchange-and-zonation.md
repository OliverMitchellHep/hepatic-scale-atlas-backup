# P02 · Sinusoidal exchange, uptake and zonation

**Level:** MRCP  **Prerequisites:** A11, P01  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook physiology, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. The structure of the sinusoid, the zones and the oxygen gradient are defined in A11 and are used here without redefinition.

## Learning outcomes
1. Explain how solutes cross from blood to hepatocyte and back, and what limits uptake.
2. Define extraction ratio and first-pass metabolism and predict how shunting and blood flow change them.
3. Describe how zonation arises and how it is regulated.
4. Predict which zone a given injury or drug will affect and why.

## 1 · Exchange across the sinusoid

**Atlas view.** Tier 3. `endothelium` On, `disse` On, `hepatocytes` On, `blood` On, `bile` On.
Buttons: **"Follow a solute from lumen to hepatocyte"** (highlight `fenestrated-endothelium`, `space-of-disse`, `sinusoidal-membrane`, `basolateral-transporter`) · **"Follow bile export"** (`canalicular-transporter`, `bile-canaliculus`) · **"Show Kupffer cells"** (`kupffer-cell`).

### 1.1 Pathway
- Plasma passes through endothelial fenestrae into the space of Disse and reaches the sinusoidal membrane of the hepatocyte; red cells stay in the lumen. Uptake occurs by diffusion and by basolateral transporters, and export into bile by canalicular transporters (P03). [T; wall structure V: Poisson 2017]
- LSECs and Kupffer cells also clear macromolecules and particles by endocytosis and phagocytosis. [T; immune role V: Kubes & Jenne 2018 for the liver as a frontline immune tissue that captures pathogens and macromolecules entering via the gut]

### 1.2 Extraction and first-pass metabolism
- **Extraction ratio** = (inflow concentration − outflow concentration) ÷ inflow concentration. High-extraction drugs are limited by hepatic blood flow, low-extraction drugs by enzyme capacity and protein binding. [T]
- **First-pass metabolism:** an oral drug absorbed into the portal vein passes through the liver before reaching the systemic circulation; portosystemic shunting bypasses this and raises systemic exposure. [T]

## 2 · Zonation

**Atlas view.** Tier 1/2 `acinar` On, `flow` On. Zonation gene overlay: [NEEDS ASSET].
Buttons: **"Show zones 1–3"** (highlight `zone-1`, `zone-2`, `zone-3`) · **"Show the oxygen gradient"** (`flow`; 60–65 → 30–35 mmHg from A11).

### 2.1 Regulation
- Zonation is set by oxygen, hormones and morphogen gradients. Wnt/β-catenin signalling is highest in perivenous cells; hepatocyte plasticity means zonal identity is dynamic. [V: Paris & Henderson 2022, review, Hepatology; detail T]
- In mouse liver, more than half of expressed genes are significantly zonated; profiles include mid-lobule peaks. Mouse data. [V: Halpern 2017; A11]

### 2.2 Functional consequences
- **Periportal:** gluconeogenesis, ureagenesis, β-oxidation, cholesterol synthesis, bile acid uptake. **Perivenous:** glycolysis, lipogenesis, glutamine synthesis and xenobiotic metabolism. [T; A11 section 3.3 for the summary]
- Because glutamine synthetase sits in a thin perivenous layer downstream of urea synthesis, it acts as a scavenger of ammonia that escapes the urea cycle (P04). [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 3 · Clinical correlation

### 3.1 Drug handling
- Shunting (TIPS, spontaneous shunts) raises systemic levels of high-extraction drugs and decreases first-pass clearance. [T]
- In cirrhosis, capillarisation (A11 section 5.3) and reduced fenestrae reduce solute transfer to hepatocytes; extraction of high-extraction drugs falls. [V: DeLeve 2015 for capillarisation; consequence T]

### 3.2 Zone-specific injury
- Paracetamol and hypoxia injure zone 3 (P05, P01). [V: EASL 2017; Henrion 2012; A11]
- The atlas paints zonal injury patterns across tiers 1–3 (`state.path.zonal`: `apap`, `ischaemic`, `congestive`, `iron`, `aih`). [Atlas]

## 4 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** A drug with a high hepatic extraction ratio has clearance mainly limited by:
A. Enzyme capacity  B. Protein binding  C. Hepatic blood flow  D. Renal function  E. Gut absorption
**Answer: C.** High-extraction drugs are flow-limited.

**Q2.** After TIPS insertion, the systemic exposure to an oral high-extraction drug is likely to:
A. Fall  B. Rise because first-pass metabolism is bypassed  C. Be unchanged  D. Fall because of increased flow  E. Be halved
**Answer: B.** Portal blood bypasses hepatocytes through the shunt.

**Q3.** Which pathway is characteristic of periportal hepatocytes?
A. Glutamine synthesis  B. Glycolysis  C. Gluconeogenesis  D. CYP2E1 metabolism  E. Lipogenesis
**Answer: C.** Perivenous cells favour glycolysis, glutamine synthesis and most xenobiotic metabolism.

**Q4.** In mouse liver, the proportion of genes significantly zonated is approximately:
A. 5%  B. 10%  C. 25%  D. More than 50%  E. 100%
**Answer: D.** (Halpern 2017.) It is mouse data.

**Q5.** Capillarisation of the sinusoid in cirrhosis would be expected to:
A. Increase fenestrae  B. Reduce fenestrae and add a basement membrane  C. Have no effect on exchange  D. Increase Kupffer cell number only  E. Abolish blood flow
**Answer: B.** Loss of fenestrae and an organised basement membrane (DeLeve 2015).
