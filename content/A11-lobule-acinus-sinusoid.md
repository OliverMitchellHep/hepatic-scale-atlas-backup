# A11 · Lobule, acinus and sinusoid

**Level:** student → MRCP  **Prerequisite:** A02 (light)  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search this session; **[T]** standard textbook histology/physiology (Sherlock, Guyton and Hall, Gray's, Blumgart), not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed.

## Learning outcomes
1. Contrast the classic lobule with Rappaport's acinus and say when each is the more useful model.
2. Identify what sits in a portal tract and at the centre of a lobule, and follow blood and bile through the parenchyma in opposite directions.
3. Define zones 1–3 and explain why the oxygen gradient makes zone 3 the most vulnerable to ischaemia.
4. Describe the sinusoidal wall, the space of Disse and the cells that occupy them.
5. Link zonal anatomy to specific injury patterns and to capillarisation and fibrosis.

### 1.1 Classic lobule
- A hexagonal prism with a central vein in the middle and portal tracts at the corners. Plates of hepatocytes radiate between them. [T]
- In humans the lobule is poorly bounded by connective tissue; septa are prominent in the pig but not in humans. [T]

### 1.2 Acinus
- Rappaport (1954) redrew the unit around the terminal portal venule and arteriole: a small volume of parenchyma centred on the portal tract axis and reaching out to the central veins on either side. [V: Rappaport 1954]
- Its zones follow distance from the inflow. This is a **functional** unit defined by blood supply, not by a histological border. [V]

### 1.3 Angioarchitectural refinement
- Matsumoto and Kawakami (1982) worked from portal venous casts: cone-shaped primary lobules, with six to eight of them forming a classic lobule. [V: search summary of abstract]

### 1.4 Which model to use
- Use the **lobule** to name patterns on a slide: centrilobular, periportal, midzonal, bridging. [T]
- Use the **acinus** to explain why: zones 1–3 are defined by oxygen and nutrient delivery. Both describe the same tissue.

## 2 · The two ends of the flow path

**Atlas view.** Tier 1. `portal` On, `artery` On, `bile` On, `central` On, `flow` On.
Buttons: **"Follow blood: portal to central"** (highlight `portal-tract`, `terminal-portal-venule`, `inlet-venule`, `central-vein`) · **"Follow bile: canaliculus to ductule"** (highlight `bile-canaliculus`, `bile-ductule`).

### 2.1 Portal tract
- Contents: a portal venule, a hepatic arteriole and a bile duct (the "triad"), plus lymphatics and nerves, in connective tissue. [T]
- The **limiting plate** is the rim of periportal hepatocytes that bounds the tract. [T]

### 2.2 Central vein
- The terminal hepatic venule. It has no muscular sheath and receives sinusoids directly. [T]

### 2.3 Countercurrent
- Blood runs **portal → central** (zone 1 to 3). Bile runs the other way, from canaliculi towards the ductules at the portal tract. [T]
- Arterial and portal blood mix at the sinusoid entrance; the arteriole supplies little volume, but it perfuses the bile duct plexus. [T]

## 3 · Zonation

**Atlas view.** Tier 2 (acinus). `acinar` On, `flow` On.
Buttons: **"Show acinar zones 1–3"** (highlight `zone-1`, `zone-2`, `zone-3`) · **"Show the oxygen gradient"** (`flow` On; caption 60–65 → 30–35 mmHg) · **"Switch to lobule view"** (tier 1).
Zonation gene/enzyme overlay: [NEEDS ASSET].

### 3.1 Zones
- **Zone 1** (periportal): first contact with incoming blood, richest in oxygen and nutrients. **Zone 3** (perivenous/pericentral): last, lowest in oxygen. **Zone 2** lies between. [V: Rappaport 1954]

### 3.2 Oxygen gradient
- Oxygen tension falls from about 60–65 mmHg in periportal blood to about 30–35 mmHg in perivenous blood. Intracellular values are about 15 mmHg lower. [V: Jungermann & Kietzmann 2000, review]

### 3.3 Metabolic division of labour
- Periportal: oxidative metabolism, gluconeogenesis, ureagenesis, β-oxidation, bile acid uptake. Perivenous: glycolysis, lipogenesis, glutamine synthesis, and most cytochrome P450 drug metabolism. [T]
- The gradient of oxygen is one modulator of this zonation; hormones and morphogen signalling (Wnt/β-catenin) are others. [V: Jungermann & Kietzmann 2000 for oxygen; Wnt: T]
- Scale of the effect: in mouse liver, more than half of expressed genes are significantly zonated, including profiles that peak in the mid-lobule. Data are mouse, not human. [V: Halpern 2017]

## 4 · The sinusoid

**Atlas view.** Tier 3 (sinusoid). `endothelium` On, `disse` On, `hepatocytes` On, `cells` On, `blood` On, `bile` On.
Buttons: **"Switch to sinusoid view"** (zoom to tier 3) · **"Show the sinusoidal wall"** (highlight `fenestrated-endothelium`, `fenestra`) · **"Show space of Disse"** (`space-of-disse`, `perisinusoidal-collagen`) · **"Show Kupffer and stellate cells"** (`kupffer-cell`, `stellate-cell`, `retinoid-droplet`) · **"Show canaliculus"** (`bile-canaliculus`, `tight-junction`).

### 4.1 Wall
- Liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) have fenestrae, no diaphragm, and no basement membrane. [V: Poisson 2017]
- Plasma passes freely to the hepatocyte surface; red cells stay in the lumen. This allows high first-pass uptake. [T]

### 4.2 Space of Disse
- The perisinusoidal space between LSECs and hepatocytes. Microvilli of the hepatocyte sinusoidal face project into it. It contains a small amount of matrix and the stellate cell. [T]

### 4.3 Cells
- **Kupffer cells**: macrophages inside the sinusoid lumen. **Stellate cells**: perisinusoidal, store retinoid, and become the matrix-producing myofibroblast on activation. [T; activation V: DeLeve 2015]
- **Hepatocytes** form plates one cell thick. The sinusoidal (basolateral) face takes up from blood; the canalicular (apical) face, sealed by tight junctions, secretes bile. [T]

## 5 · Clinical correlation

### 5.1 Why zone 3 is the injury zone for ischaemia and toxins
- Lowest oxygen tension plus the richest cytochrome P450 complement makes zone 3 vulnerable to both hypoperfusion and reactive drug metabolites. [V: oxygen values, Jungermann 2000; P450 zonation: T]
- **Hypoxic (ischaemic) hepatitis:** abrupt, massive aminotransferase rise from centrilobular necrosis; cardiac, respiratory failure and septic shock account for over 90% of cases. [V: Henrion 2012, review]
- **Paracetamol:** N-acetylcysteine is indicated in all paracetamol overdoses. [EASL ALF CPG 2017, grade [UNVERIFIED]; the guideline is over 5 years old, so check for a newer version and for current UK treatment-line advice] The zone 3 localisation of injury follows from the zonation above. [T]
- **Congestion:** right heart failure or hepatic vein outflow obstruction dilates zone 3 sinusoids (nutmeg liver). [T]

### 5.2 Why the pattern matters at the bench
- **Interface hepatitis** breaches the limiting plate from the portal tract (zone 1 edge): autoimmune and viral hepatitis. [T]
- **Iron** in hereditary haemochromatosis loads periportal hepatocytes first. [T]
- **Yellow fever** causes midzonal (zone 2) necrosis. [T]
- The atlas paints each of these on the lobule, acinus and sinusoid: **"Paracetamol"**, **"Ischaemic"**, **"Congestive"**, **"Iron (HH)"**, **"Interface (AIH)"** buttons set `state.path.zonal` to `apap`, `ischaemic`, `congestive`, `iron`, `aih`.

### 5.3 Capillarisation and fibrosis
- Loss of LSEC fenestrae and formation of an organised basement membrane (capillarisation) precedes fibrosis and permits stellate cell activation. [V: DeLeve 2015, review]
- In MASLD the earliest fibrosis is perisinusoidal in zone 3, and is what the NASH CRN fibrosis stage 1 describes. [V: Kleiner 2005 for the scoring system; zone 3 wording T]

## 6 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which best describes Rappaport's acinus?
A. A hexagon with a central vein and six portal tracts at its corners
B. A functional unit centred on the portal tract axis, with zones defined by distance from inflow
C. A cone of parenchyma drained by one hepatic vein
D. The space between two adjacent portal tracts
E. A unit bounded by connective tissue septa
**Answer: B.** The acinus is defined by blood supply. A describes the classic lobule; E does not apply in human liver.

**Q2.** Which zone has the lowest oxygen tension, and approximately what value?
A. Zone 1, 30–35 mmHg  B. Zone 2, 60–65 mmHg  C. Zone 3, 30–35 mmHg  D. Zone 3, 60–65 mmHg  E. Zone 1, 60–65 mmHg but with lowest flow
**Answer: C.** Oxygen tension falls from about 60–65 mmHg periportally to about 30–35 mmHg perivenously (Jungermann & Kietzmann 2000).

**Q3.** A patient develops ALT 4,200 IU/L after cardiogenic shock, and falls to 600 IU/L within four days. Which histology is most likely?
A. Interface hepatitis  B. Periportal necrosis  C. Centrilobular (zone 3) necrosis  D. Bridging fibrosis  E. Macrovesicular steatosis
**Answer: C.** Ischaemic hepatitis produces centrilobular necrosis. Interface hepatitis suggests autoimmune or viral disease, and steatosis and fibrosis are not acute lesions.

**Q4.** Which feature is characteristic of the sinusoidal endothelium?
A. Continuous tight junctions and a thick basement membrane  B. Diaphragmed fenestrae  C. Fenestrae without a diaphragm and without a basement membrane  D. Kupffer cells lining the space of Disse  E. Smooth muscle cuff
**Answer: C.** LSECs have open fenestrae with no diaphragm and no basement membrane. Kupffer cells sit in the lumen, stellate cells in the space of Disse.

**Q5.** In early MASLD fibrosis, which describes the earliest lesion?
A. Portal-tract fibrosis only  B. Perisinusoidal fibrosis in zone 3  C. Bridging septa between portal tracts  D. Fibrosis of the central vein wall alone  E. Fibrosis in zone 1 with sparing of zone 3
**Answer: B.** NASH CRN stage 1 is perisinusoidal or periportal fibrosis, with zone 3 perisinusoidal the typical first pattern. **[UNVERIFIED wording of stage 1 definition; check Kleiner 2005 Table.]**
