# A04 · Biliary tree, gallbladder and variants

## Learning outcomes
1. Name the intrahepatic and extrahepatic ducts from canaliculus to ampulla, and state which segments each duct drains.
2. Describe the gallbladder's parts, blood supply and the cystohepatic triangle.
3. State the common intrahepatic and cystic duct variants with cited prevalence.
4. Apply the anatomy to bile duct injury, perihilar cholangiocarcinoma staging and MRCP interpretation.

## 1 · The duct hierarchy

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior. `biliary` On, `parenchyma` Ghost, `portal` Ghost, `artery` Ghost.
Buttons: **"Intrahepatic to extrahepatic"** (highlight `segmental-bile-duct`, `intrahepatic-bile-ducts`, `right-hepatic-duct`, `left-hepatic-duct`, `common-hepatic-duct`) · **"Whole extrahepatic tree"** (`common-bile-duct`, `cystic-duct`, `gallbladder`, `ampulla-of-vater`).

### 1.1 Intrahepatic
- Canaliculi (the hepatocyte apical membrane; A11) drain into canals of Hering, interlobular ducts and then segmental ducts, which run with the portal pedicles. [T]
- Segmental ducts unite as sectoral ducts and then the right and left hepatic ducts. The left duct is longer and more horizontal than the right. [T]

### 1.2 Confluence and extrahepatic ducts
- The right and left hepatic ducts join at the hilum to form the common hepatic duct (CHD), usually just outside the liver. The cystic duct joins the CHD to form the common bile duct (CBD), which descends in the hepatoduodenal ligament to the ampulla. [T]
- The atlas models the CBD entering the pancreatic head from above and staying within it to the ampulla. [Atlas note, v46]

### 1.3 Biliary blood supply
- Ducts have an arterial plexus fed by the hepatic artery; the atlas teaching point says the biliary tree is supplied by the hepatic artery alone. [T; the arterial plexus supply is textbook; no source verified, **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 2 · Gallbladder and cystohepatic triangle

**Atlas view.** Tier 0, anterior and inferior. `biliary` On, `artery` On, `parenchyma` Ghost.
Buttons: **"Gallbladder parts"** (highlight `gallbladder`) · **"Cystic duct and cystic artery"** (`cystic-duct`, `cystic-artery`) · **"Gallbladder fossa"** (camera inferior). Calot's triangle and Hartmann's pouch: [NEEDS ASSET].

### 2.1 Parts
- Fundus, body, infundibulum (Hartmann's pouch when a gallstone or dilatation is present) and neck, which continues into the cystic duct with its spiral folds. [T]
- The gallbladder lies in a fossa on the visceral surface between segments IVb and V. [T]

### 2.2 Cystohepatic triangle and Calot's triangle
- The **cystohepatic triangle** is bounded by the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the inferior edge of the liver, and contains the cystic artery and often a right hepatic artery loop. **Calot's triangle** in its original sense has the cystic artery as the upper border, not the liver edge. The terms are used loosely; state which you mean. [T]

### 2.3 Critical view of safety
- Strasberg introduced the **critical view of safety** in 1995 to identify the cystic duct and artery before division. [V: Strasberg 1995]
- SAGES/multi-society guideline (2020) recommends using the critical view of safety for identification of the cystic duct and artery in laparoscopic cholecystectomy; where it cannot be achieved, consider subtotal cholecystectomy or a fundus-first approach. [V: Brunt 2020; guideline is over 5 years old, check for update; grades **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 3 · Variants

**Atlas view.** Variants are not modelled in the atlas. [NEEDS ASSET: right posterior duct into the left duct; triple confluence; posterior duct into the CHD or cystic duct; accessory duct.]

### 3.1 Intrahepatic ducts (300 living donors)
- Typical anatomy in **63%**, atypical in **37%**. [V: Choi 2003, intraoperative cholangiography in 300 consecutive donors]
- **Right posterior segmental duct into the left hepatic duct: 11%. Triple confluence of right anterior, right posterior and left ducts: 10%. Right posterior duct into the CHD: 6%. Right posterior duct into the cystic duct: 2%. Accessory duct to the CHD or right duct: 5%.** [V: Choi 2003]
- An MR cholangiographic review reports the right posterior duct draining into the left hepatic duct as the most common variant, in 13–19% of the population, and accessory hepatic ducts in about 2%. [V: Mortelé & Ros 2001, pictorial essay; figures from a search summary]
- Figures differ between studies because of population and method (donors vs MRCP). Cite the study when you quote a number.

### 3.2 Cystic duct
- The cystic duct may join low, run parallel to the CHD or spiral before joining, and cystic duct anomalies matter for cystic duct stump management. [T; prevalence: **[UNVERIFIED]**, not sourced here]

## 4 · Clinical correlation

### 4.1 Bile duct injury
- Injury usually results from misidentifying the CBD or a right sectoral duct as the cystic duct, more likely with an aberrant right posterior duct or a short, inflamed cystic duct. [V: mechanism from Strasberg 1995 analysis of the problem; the link to right posterior duct anomalies is T]
- Practical rule from the SAGES guideline: obtain the critical view of safety, and bail out (subtotal cholecystectomy) if it cannot be achieved. [V: Brunt 2020]

### 4.2 Perihilar cholangiocarcinoma
- The **Bismuth–Corlette** classification describes the extent of ductal involvement of hilar tumours (types I–IV, by relation to the confluence and to the right and left ducts) and guides the extent of resection. [V: Bismuth & Corlette 1975 for the classification and its purpose; type definitions T]
- Duct variants (for example a right posterior duct joining the left duct) can change which side is resectable and where the duct must be transected. [T]

### 4.3 Sclerosing cholangitis and MRCP
- MRCP is the preferred diagnostic test in adult patients with elevated cholestatic markers when large-duct PSC is suspected (EASL 2022: LoE 2, strong recommendation, 93% consensus). Pooled sensitivity and specificity were 86% and 94%. [V: EASL 2022 CPG; guideline is 4 years old, within the 5-year window]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which best describes the most common variant of the intrahepatic ducts in the Choi 2003 donor series?
A. Triple confluence  B. Right posterior duct into the left hepatic duct  C. Right posterior duct into the cystic duct  D. Accessory duct  E. Left duct into the CHD
**Answer: B.** The right posterior segmental duct drained into the left hepatic duct in 11%, more often than triple confluence (10%). Typical anatomy was present in 63%.

**Q2.** During laparoscopic cholecystectomy the critical view of safety requires:
A. Division of the cystic artery before the duct  B. Two structures only entering the gallbladder, with the lower gallbladder separated from the cystic plate  C. Cholangiography in all cases  D. Ligation of the hepatic artery  E. A top-down approach
**Answer: B.** The critical view is achieved when the hepatocystic triangle is cleared of fat and fibrous tissue, the lower third of the gallbladder is separated from the cystic plate, and two and only two structures are seen entering the gallbladder. **[T: criteria wording; cited to Strasberg 1995, not verified here.]**

**Q3.** The Bismuth–Corlette classification is used to:
A. Grade gallstone disease  B. Describe the extent of hilar bile duct tumours  C. Classify choledochal cysts  D. Stage PSC  E. Define the biliary blood supply
**Answer: B.** It describes the involvement of the confluence and the right and left ducts in perihilar cholangiocarcinoma. Choledochal cysts use the Todani classification.

**Q4.** According to EASL 2022 guidance, which is the preferred diagnostic test for large-duct PSC in an adult with cholestatic enzymes?
A. ERCP  B. MRCP  C. Liver biopsy  D. Ultrasound  E. HIDA scan
**Answer: B.** MRCP (LoE 2, strong recommendation).

**Q5.** A bile duct injury is most likely to occur in which situation?
A. Short, inflamed cystic duct with an unrecognised right posterior duct joining the CHD  B. Long cystic duct  C. Low cystic duct insertion  D. Normal anatomy  E. Double gallbladder
**Answer: A.** Misidentification of duct anatomy is the key mechanism; aberrant ducts and severe inflammation make it more likely. The critical view of safety addresses this. **[T for the specific risk factors; V for the CVS principle.]**
