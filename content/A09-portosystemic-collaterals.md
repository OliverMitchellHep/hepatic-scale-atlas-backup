# A09 · Portosystemic collateral anatomy

**Level:** MRCP → registrar  **Prerequisites:** A03  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** checked against a source found by web search; **[T]** standard textbook anatomy, not opened this session; **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed. Portal pressure physiology is in P07; this module covers the routes.

## Learning outcomes
1. Name the classic sites of portosystemic anastomosis and the veins at each.
2. Trace gastro-oesophageal varices from their inflow to their outflow, and classify gastric varices.
3. Identify the spontaneous splenorenal and paraumbilical routes and other spontaneous shunts on imaging.
4. Recognise ectopic varices and state how common they are.

### 1.1 Four textbook sites
| Site | Portal side | Systemic side |
|---|---|---|
| Lower oesophagus/gastric cardia | Left gastric vein | Oesophageal veins → azygos/hemiazygos |
| Anorectal | Superior rectal (via IMV) | Middle and inferior rectal (internal iliac, pudendal) |
| Umbilicus | Paraumbilical veins (left portal vein) | Superficial epigastric veins |
| Retroperitoneal/bare area | Colonic and splenic tributaries | Renal, lumbar, phrenic veins |
[T]

### 1.2 Splenorenal and other spontaneous shunts
- The splenic vein or its tributaries connect to the left renal vein directly or through the adrenal and gonadal veins. [T]
- Spontaneous portosystemic shunts can be large enough to divert most portal flow, which is the basis of shunt-related hepatic encephalopathy. [T]

## 2 · Gastro-oesophageal varices

**Atlas view.** Tier 0. `stomach` On, `esophagus` On, `portal` On, `hepaticVein` Ghost. Buttons as above.

### 2.1 Flow path
- Inflow: left gastric and short gastric veins. Outflow: submucosal oesophageal veins → periesophageal and azygos veins. Increased flow and pressure in the palisade and perforating veins of the lower oesophagus produce varices at the gastro-oesophageal junction. [T]

### 2.2 Sarin classification of gastric varices
- **GOV1** (oesophageal varices extending along the lesser curve), **GOV2** (along the fundus), **IGV1** (isolated in the fundus), **IGV2** (isolated elsewhere). [V: Sarin classification, from search summaries]
- In Sarin's series GOV1 accounted for about 75% of gastric varices, GOV2 21%, IGV1 under 2% and IGV2 4%. IGV1 bleeds most, then GOV2. [V: search summary of Sarin 1992; **[UNVERIFIED]** figures until checked against the paper]
- The 1-year bleeding risk of gastric varices has been reported at about 10–16%. [V: secondary summary; **[UNVERIFIED]**]

## 3 · Ectopic varices

### 3.1 Prevalence and sites
- Ectopic varices account for 1–5% of variceal bleeding. They occur in the duodenum, jejunum and ileum, colon, rectum, stoma and peritoneum; one summary reports about 17% in the duodenum, 14% in the colon, 8% in the rectum and 9% in the peritoneum among ectopic varices. [V: review-level sources found by search; authors not identified, **[UNVERIFIED citation]**]
- They are more common in extrahepatic portal hypertension (reported 20–30%) than in cirrhosis (1–3%). [V: same sources]
- Stomal varices arise at the mucocutaneous junction of an enterostomy in patients with portal hypertension. [V: same sources; T]

## 4 · Clinical correlation

### 4.1 Embolisation and shunts
- Baveno VIII (2026) states that embolisation of large spontaneous portosystemic shunts should be considered in patients with recurrent or persistent hepatic encephalopathy despite optimal medical therapy, particularly when liver function is preserved, and that TIPS may be combined with embolisation to control bleeding from gastric or ectopic varices when flow remains diverted to collaterals. [V: search summaries of Baveno VIII; content **[UNVERIFIED]** until the text is read]
- Baveno VII (2022) is superseded. [V]

### 4.2 Caput medusae, paraumbilical vein and Cruveilhier–Baumgarten
- A recanalised paraumbilical vein in the falciform ligament, seen on ultrasound or CT, indicates portal hypertension; caput medusae is the abdominal wall manifestation. [T]

## 5 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which vein is the main portal-side vessel supplying oesophageal varices?
A. Inferior mesenteric  B. Left gastric  C. Cystic  D. Right gastric  E. Superior rectal
**Answer: B.** The left gastric vein drains to the portal trunk or splenic vein and feeds the submucosal oesophageal plexus.

**Q2.** Which collateral connects the splenic and renal venous systems?
A. Paraumbilical  B. Splenorenal shunt  C. Azygos  D. Haemorrhoidal  E. Cystic
**Answer: B.**

**Q3.** In Sarin's classification, isolated fundal gastric varices are:
A. GOV1  B. GOV2  C. IGV1  D. IGV2  E. Ectopic
**Answer: C.** GOV1 and GOV2 are contiguous with oesophageal varices; IGV1 is fundal.

**Q4.** Ectopic varices account for approximately what proportion of variceal bleeding?
A. Under 1%  B. 1–5%  C. 10–15%  D. 25%  E. Over 50%
**Answer: B.** Search summaries report 1–5%. **[UNVERIFIED]**

**Q5.** A patient with recurrent hepatic encephalopathy and preserved liver function has a large spontaneous splenorenal shunt. Which option is consistent with Baveno VIII as summarised?
A. Ignore the shunt  B. Consider embolisation of the large shunt and transplant discussion  C. Stop lactulose  D. Splenectomy  E. Beta-blocker alone
**Answer: B.** Embolisation should be considered when HE persists despite optimal therapy. **[UNVERIFIED wording; check the Baveno VIII text.]**
