# P03 · Bile formation and the enterohepatic circulation

**Level:** MRCP  **Prerequisites:** A04, A11, P02  **Draft:** 29 Sept 2026

## Learning outcomes
1. Describe canalicular bile formation and the bile-acid-dependent and independent fractions.
2. Name the canalicular and basolateral transporters and the disease each causes when defective.
3. Trace the enterohepatic circulation of bile acids and its regulation by FXR/FGF19.
4. Explain gallbladder filling, concentration and CCK-mediated emptying with sphincter coordination.

## 1 · Canalicular secretion

**Atlas view.** Tier 4 (hepatocyte) `transporters` On; tier 3 `bile` On.
Buttons: **"Show canalicular transporters"** (highlight `canalicular-transporter`, `canalicular-membrane`, `bile-canaliculus`, `tight-junction`) · **"Show basolateral transporters"** (`basolateral-transporter`, `sinusoidal-membrane`).

### 1.1 Two fractions
- Roughly 60% of hepatocellular bile flow is bile-acid-dependent, driven by the bile salt export pump (BSEP, ABCB11); about 40% is bile-acid-independent, depending on glutathione, bicarbonate and other organic anions, including via MRP2 (ABCC2). [V: Boyer 2013, review]
- BSEP secretes monovalent conjugated bile acids; MRP2 secretes bilirubin conjugates, phase II conjugates and divalent bile acids. [V: Boyer 2013]
- Ductular secretion (bicarbonate-rich, secretin-stimulated) modifies bile downstream. [T]

### 1.2 Transporters and disease
| Transporter | Function | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| NTCP (SLC10A1), basolateral | Conjugated bile acid uptake | Hypercholanaemia |
| BSEP (ABCB11), canalicular | Bile acid export | PFIC2, benign recurrent intrahepatic cholestasis |
| MDR3 (ABCB4), canalicular | Phospholipid export | PFIC3, low-phospholipid-associated cholelithiasis |
| MRP2 (ABCC2), canalicular | Conjugated bilirubin export | Dubin–Johnson syndrome |
[T; **[UNVERIFIED]** until checked against a hepatology reference]

## 2 · Enterohepatic circulation

**Atlas view.** Tier 0. `biliary` On, `duodenum` On, `portal` On. Terminal ileum and the portal return: [NEEDS ASSET].
Buttons: **"Bile to the duodenum"** (highlight `common-bile-duct`, `ampulla-of-vater`, `duodenum`) · **"Return via the portal vein"** (`portal-trunk`, `superior-mesenteric-vein`). Ileal reabsorption: [NEEDS ASSET].

### 2.1 Cycle
- Conjugated bile acids are secreted into bile, stored and released into the duodenum, aid fat digestion and absorption, and are mostly reabsorbed in the ileum by the apical sodium-dependent bile acid transporter (ASBT), then returned to the liver in portal blood. [V: Chiang 2013, review]
- Bile acids entering ileal enterocytes activate FXR, which induces FGF19; FGF19 signals through FGFR4/β-klotho in the liver to inhibit CYP7A1, the rate-limiting step of bile acid synthesis. [V: Chiang 2013 and related reviews]
- The proportion reabsorbed is high and the pool cycles several times a day. [T; figures **[UNVERIFIED]**]

### 2.2 Gallbladder and sphincter
- Between meals bile is diverted to the gallbladder through the cystic duct while the sphincter of Oddi is contracted; the gallbladder concentrates bile by absorbing water and electrolytes. [T]
- CCK from duodenal enteroendocrine cells contracts the gallbladder and relaxes the sphincter after a fat-containing meal. [T]

## 3 · Clinical correlation

### 3.1 Cholestasis
- Impaired secretion of bile acids or reduced duct flow causes cholestasis, with pruritus and raised ALP/GGT (P08). [T]
- Guidelines: EASL PBC CPG **2017** (J Hepatol 67:145–172) is over 5 years old, and no newer EASL PBC CPG was found by search; EASL sclerosing cholangitis **2022** is current. [V: existence, dates]

### 3.2 Gallstones
- Gallstone pathogenesis involves cholesterol supersaturation, reduced gallbladder motility and nucleation. [T]
- The EASL gallstone CPG (2016, J Hepatol 65:146–181) is over 5 years old; no update was found by search. [V]

### 3.3 Bile acid diarrhoea
- Ileal disease or resection, or reduced FGF19 feedback, increases bile acid delivery to the colon. [T; **[UNVERIFIED]**; check current BSG guidance]

## 4 · Self-assessment (SBA)

**Q1.** Which transporter is the main canalicular exporter of conjugated monovalent bile acids?
A. NTCP  B. BSEP  C. MRP2  D. MDR3  E. ASBT
**Answer: B.** BSEP (ABCB11). NTCP is the basolateral uptake transporter; ASBT is ileal.

**Q2.** Which defect causes Dubin–Johnson syndrome?
A. MRP2  B. BSEP  C. UGT1A1  D. NTCP  E. FIC1
**Answer: A.** MRP2 secretes conjugated bilirubin. **[T; UNVERIFIED]**

**Q3.** FGF19 acts on the liver to:
A. Increase CYP7A1  B. Inhibit CYP7A1  C. Increase BSEP  D. Reduce NTCP only  E. Increase bile flow
**Answer: B.** Ileal FXR-induced FGF19 signals via FGFR4/β-klotho to inhibit CYP7A1.

**Q4.** Approximately what fraction of hepatocellular bile flow is bile-acid-dependent?
A. 10%  B. 30%  C. 60%  D. 90%  E. 100%
**Answer: C.** About 60% (Boyer 2013).

**Q5.** Which hormone contracts the gallbladder and relaxes the sphincter of Oddi after a fatty meal?
A. Secretin  B. Gastrin  C. CCK  D. Somatostatin  E. Glucagon
**Answer: C.**
