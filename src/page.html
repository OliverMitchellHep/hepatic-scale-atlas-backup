                            Liver biopsy
                            When this route is chosen
                                HVPG measurement
                                1. 1 · CatheterisationA balloon catheter is passed from the right internal jugular vein through the right atrium and down the IVC to the hepatic vein ostia under fluoroscopy.
                                2. 2 · Free hepatic venous pressureThe tip is advanced 2–4 cm into the right hepatic vein with the balloon deflated. FHVP approximates IVC pressure and is the zero reference that cancels intra-abdominal pressure.
                                3. 3 · Wedged hepatic venous pressureThe balloon is inflated to occlude the vein. The static column reflects sinusoidal pressure, which approximates portal pressure in sinusoidal (e.g. cirrhotic) portal hypertension. Readings are taken once stable, in triplicate.
                                FHVP— WHVP— HVPG = WHVP − FHVP—
                                Stage 3 · What the wedged pressure measures
                                Why the wedge reflects portal pressure

                                Inflating the balloon stops outflow from that segment of the hepatic vein. With no flow there is no pressure drop along the static column of blood, so the catheter tip reads the pressure where that column meets moving blood: the hepatic sinusoids. In cirrhosis the sinusoids are in free continuity with the portal venules (little pre-sinusoidal resistance), so the wedged pressure approximates portal venous pressure.

                                Why a gradient, not the wedge alone

                                The wedged pressure is an absolute value. It also contains everything that raises pressure around and downstream of the liver: intra-abdominal pressure (tense ascites), IVC and right atrial pressure (heart failure, tricuspid regurgitation), and the level at which the transducer was zeroed. The free hepatic venous pressure, measured in the same vein in the same sitting, carries the same offsets, so subtracting it cancels them.

                                HVPG = WHVP − FHVP is what remains: the pressure drop across the sinusoidal bed, set by intrahepatic resistance and portal inflow. That is why the thresholds (≥ 10 mmHg clinically significant; ≥ 12 mmHg bleeding risk) are defined for the gradient. A pre-sinusoidal block (e.g. portal vein thrombosis) raises portal pressure without raising the wedge, so HVPG underestimates it.
