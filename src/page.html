          Pancreatic duct drainage
          Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. A head tumour encases the intrapancreatic bile duct and the main pancreatic duct (double duct sign) and distends the gallbladder; a tail tumour leaves the bile duct alone. NICE NG85: resectable disease with jaundice goes straight to surgery without preoperative drainage unless surgery must wait (then an endoscopic metal stent); unresectable disease is palliated with an endoscopic metal stent. PTC is for when ERCP fails.
          Pancreatitis
          Acute: an oedematous, inflamed gland with peripancreatic fluid and fat stranding. Chronic: an atrophic, calcified gland with a dilated, irregular main duct and pseudocysts. Splenic vein thrombosis causes left-sided (sinistral) portal hypertension with isolated gastric varices. NICE NG104.

                HVPG measurement
                1. 1 · CatheterisationA balloon catheter is passed from the right internal jugular vein through the right atrium and down the IVC to the hepatic vein ostia under fluoroscopy.
                2. 2 · Free hepatic venous pressureThe tip is advanced 2–4 cm into the right hepatic vein with the balloon deflated. FHVP approximates IVC pressure and is the zero reference that cancels intra-abdominal pressure.
                3. 3 · Wedged hepatic venous pressureThe balloon is inflated to occlude the vein. The static column reflects sinusoidal pressure, which approximates portal pressure in sinusoidal (e.g. cirrhotic) portal hypertension. Readings are taken once stable, in triplicate.
                FHVP— WHVP— HVPG = WHVP − FHVP—
